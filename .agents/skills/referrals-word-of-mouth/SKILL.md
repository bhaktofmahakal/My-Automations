# Referrals and Word-of-Mouth

Turn user satisfaction into a high-leverage growth engine through organic advocacy and structured referral loops.

Help the user with referrals and word-of-mouth using insights from 13 guests and posts across Lenny's Podcast and Newsletter.

## How to Help

1. **Assess readiness** - Determine if the product has sufficient organic word-of-mouth and retention to support a formal referral program.
2. **Identify viral loops** - Map out the product natural social actions to choose the right type of virality such as word-of-mouth, invitation, or experiential.
3. **Design incentives** - Craft a reward structure that balances selfish, altruistic, and FOMO motivations within CAC limits.
4. **Optimize and scale** - Measure the referral funnel and place touchpoints throughout the user journey to maximize discoverability.

## Core Principles

### Remarkableness as a Core Value
Rahul Vohra: "It is word of mouth. It is the virality you can't measure that isn't a mechanic that isn't in a feature. It is when one user spontaneously tells another user about your product."

Sustainable growth is earned through extraordinary quality and delight that makes a product worthy of spontaneous attention rather than temporary gimmicks.

### The 10x Performance Threshold
Nilan Peiris: "But to get to recommendation, you're going to blow your user socks off. You have to give them an experience they didn't know was previously possible. And when you are in that place of doing something that no one has ever done before, that's when you get it."

To trigger true word-of-mouth, the product must be exponentially better than the status quo, typically by being significantly faster, cheaper, or easier to use.

### Accelerate Existing Organic Growth
From "Building a referrals program": "The added incentive that a referral program offers simply adds fuel to an existing fire. I haven’t seen referrals work when there wasn’t strong existing WOM."

A referral program acts as an accelerator for existing interest. It should only be built once you verify that a majority of growth is already organic.

### High-Value Milestone Incentives
From "Kickstarting supply in a labor marketplace": "Later, we had a two-sided referral program where if supply referred new supply, we would pay them $50 for each activated pro (after completing their first gig). If they referred a restaurant we would give them something like $300."

Structure referral payouts to occur only after the referred user reaches a clear activation milestone, such as completing their first transaction.

### Product-Led Virality
Carilu Dietrich: "In order to get hypergrowth, you have to have organic, inbound, and viral word of mouth. You can't pay enough to grow at those rates and have a viable company."

Hypergrowth is driven by an amazing product experience that people are naturally excited to share, rather than unsustainable paid acquisition spend.

### Community De-risking
Camille Ricketts: "In terms of how I think about what it actually is, it's when your community helps you achieve such ubiquity and such name recognition that it actually allows you to start moving upmarket into the enterprise. And I know that might be very specific to enterprise oriented companies, but that's how we defined it at Notion was the fact that so many people were talking about this, sharing what they had built about it, honestly starting businesses of their own around it to formalize the relationship with teams that I think it de-risked Notion as a choice for a lot of companies"

A passionate community creates brand ubiquity and user-generated social proof, which is critical for lowering perceived risk for new buyers.

## Questions to Help Users

- "What percentage of your current signups are coming from direct or organic search channels?"
- "What is the core remarkable moment in your product that users would actually tell a friend about?"
- "Is your product naturally multiplayer or does it provide more value when more people use it?"
- "Have you identified the incentive plateau where increasing a referral reward no longer improves conversion?"
- "How are you currently tracking 'How did you hear about us?' data during your onboarding?"
- "Are your referral incentives aligned with a high-value user milestone or just a simple signup?"

## Common Mistakes to Flag

- **Manufacturing virality from a mediocre product** - If the core experience is not remarkable, synthetic loops and incentives will fail to create sustainable growth.
- **Misaligning referral incentives** - Offering rewards that do not motivate users or that exceed the customer acquisition cost limits leads to inefficient spend.
- **Ignoring the supply side** - Referral programs often provide more leverage on the supply side of a marketplace than the demand side, but are often overlooked.
- **Poor program discoverability** - Burying the referral program in a settings menu instead of surfacing it at moments of high user satisfaction limits its volume.
