## Bob Moesta

> "And so the real heart of the method of Jobs to Be Done is understanding the causation of what pushes people to say, 'Today's the day I got to do something different.'"

**Insight:** Customer interviews should focus on uncovering the specific causal events and contextual triggers that led to a change in behavior.

**Tactical advice:**
- Deep dive into the 'struggling moment' to identify the specific context that makes seemingly irrational behavior rational.
- Reconstruct the timeline of events leading up to a purchase to find the true 'push' from the previous solution.
- Identify the 'four forces' during interviews: the push of the situation, the pull of the new solution, the anxiety of change, and the habit of the present.

*Source: [The ultimate guide to JTBD | Bob Moesta (co-creator of the framework)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xQV7HVyAJjc) @ 00:18:30*

## Jag Duggal

> "You make sure that you focus on customer discovery before you start building. You make sure that that discovery is focused not simply on asking the customer, but on innovating on their behalf."

## Lenny Rachitsky

> "Don’t just read reports from researchers on your team; instead attend user research sessions to get firsthand exposure to user experiences and reactions. What’s important is to pay attention to micro details and ask yourself why people react to your product the way they do."

**Insight:** Directly observing user behavior provides a firsthand understanding of emotional and functional needs that research reports often overlook.

**Tactical advice:**
- Attend user research sessions two to four times per month to observe reactions firsthand.
- Ask open-ended questions like "What are you thinking right now?" to uncover the "why" behind user actions.
- Spot moments of hesitation, confusion, or excitement by paying close attention to facial expressions.

*Source: [How to develop product sense](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/how-to-develop-product-sense)*

> "Pick the people you talk to carefully. Figuring out the target audience for your product is very much part of this search, so be careful getting discouraged by talking to the wrong group of people."

**Insight:** Customer discovery should focus on understanding the current alternative solutions and specific annoyances to identify where a new product can create differentiated value.

**Tactical advice:**
- Ask potential users how they are currently solving the problem and what specifically annoys them about that approach.
- Use the 'magic wand' question to uncover the user's ideal solution without the constraints of current technology.

> "When talking to people, look for two things: pain and pull. Pain tells you there’s an opportunity to solve a problem. Pull tells you that you’re actually solving the problem."

**Insight:** Effective customer discovery ignores polite encouragement and looks specifically for signs of intense pain or the natural pull of a working solution.

**Tactical advice:**
- Search for signs of "pull," such as people paying money or continuing to use a bad prototype.
- Listen for "pain" signals, like an emotional hatred for incumbent products.
- Distinguish between friends rooting for your success and actual market validation.

> "Don’t ask users if something is working—watch them do it. That will give you a much better sense of their pain points."

**Insight:** Observational research reveals friction points and pain points that users often cannot articulate or might misrepresent when asked directly.

**Tactical advice:**
- Conduct sessions where you silently observe users interacting with your product to see where they struggle.
- Look for user indifference rather than hate, as indifferent users are the most common and often signal a lack of utility.
- Iterate on small improvements and re-test with the same users, as they are usually willing to give products a second chance.

## Shaun Clowes

> "Avoid availability or confirmation bias. Most of the time people go talk to the people they always talk to and they learn nothing particularly new. They don't synthesize the results that they got from that conversation. They don't seek out the counterfactual, they don't seek out the proof that they're wrong."

**Insight:** To avoid confirmation bias, you must intentionally seek out new customer perspectives and data points that disprove your current assumptions.

**Tactical advice:**
- Interview between 7 and 14 people to find the sweet spot where learning is maximized before hitting diminishing returns.
- Actively seek out counterfactuals and proof that your hypothesis is wrong during synthesis.
- Compare actual product usage data against what customers tell you in conversations to find discrepancies.

*Source: [Why great AI products are all about the data | Shaun Clowes (CPO Confluent, ex-Salesforce, Atlassian)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yVS1gTAQYSU) @ 00:11:30*

## Uri Levine

**Insight:** Designing for the 'early majority' requires extreme simplicity, as this group is often more resistant to technological change than early adopters.

**Tactical advice:**
- Observe new and churned users to identify where your product lacks simplicity.
- Recognize that different user segments will interact with your product in wildly distinct ways.
- Optimize for ease of use to overcome the adoption friction felt by mainstream users.
