## Frameworks

### 0-to-1 Corporate Incubation Model (Noam Lovinsky)
How to structure an internal incubator to avoid adverse selection and corporate bureaucracy.

How it works: Key pillars: 1. Remove standard performance management cycles (e.g., bi-annual reviews). 2. Allow teams to choose their own infrastructure rather than forcing corporate tech stacks. 3. Adjust time horizons and upside incentives to match startup realities rather than corporate promo cycles.

### 5-Step AI Adoption Framework (25 proven tactics to accelerate AI adoption at your company)
A comprehensive framework with 5 recurring steps that AI-forward companies use to supercharge employee AI adoption, each with 5 specific tactics (25 total)

How it works: Step 1: Explain the How — (a) Include specific tactics in your memo, (b) Declare a 'code red' moment, (c) Define what 'embracing AI' means, (d) Embed with individual teams, (e) Lead by example in real time.

Step 2: Track and Reward Adoption — (a) Make AI adoption part of performance reviews, (b) Publish AI usage by team, (c) Track team-specific impact, (d) Use proxy metrics for productivity, (e) Make it a daily habit with challenges.

Step 3: Cut the Red Tape — (a) Create an AI learning budget, (b) Assign a lead to expedite approvals, (c) Give employees time to tinker, (d) Provide multiple tool options, (e) Embrace internal enthusiasm by letting employees nominate tools.

Step 4: Turn AI Enthusiasts into Teachers — (a) Run AI learning villages, (b) Host FriAIdays and show-and-tells, (c) Create an AI SWAT team, (d) Host regular demos and hackathons, (e) Make 'upleveling others' part of promotion criteria.

Step 5: Prioritize High-Impact Tasks — (a) Automate support triage workflows, (b) Streamline performance review prep, (c) Create content with AI, (d) Build AI personas, (e) Empower employees to build internal AI tools.

### AI Adoption Input/Output Tracking Model (25 proven tactics to accelerate AI adoption at your company)
A framework for tracking AI adoption using both leading indicators (inputs) and lagging indicators (outputs) to connect usage to business impact

How it works: Track AI adoption as:

Inputs (Leading Indicators / Who's using AI):
- Number of AI power users per team (5+ actions per week)
- AI tool usage rates by team (published transparently)
- Daily usage streaks

Outputs (Lagging Indicators / What business value it's creating):
- Team-specific impact metrics (e.g., 10 hours saved per rep per week for sales)
- Proxy metrics for productivity (e.g., merged pull requests for engineering — Intercom saw ~20% YoY improvement)
- Revenue impact (e.g., turning support tickets into sales conversations)
- Content/output velocity (e.g., Duolingo went from 100 courses in 12 years to 150 courses in 12 months)

### AI Adoption Maturity Signals (25 proven tactics to accelerate AI adoption at your company)
How to know when AI adoption is truly real vs. theater at your company

How it works: True AI adoption happens when:
- Teams become skeptical of flashy demos
- Employees demand to see rigor behind AI products
- People start asking about accuracy metrics
- Teams want to see evaluation frameworks
- Failure modes are discussed proactively

AI theater looks like:
- Flashy demos without data
- PMs stumbling when asked about evaluations
- No ground-truth datasets
- No plan for model failure
- Vague AI mandates without specific tactics

### Ally Mapping for Organizational Change (Turning around an underperforming team - Issue 17)
A framework for categorizing stakeholders into three groups when driving change on a team.

How it works: When you determine what needs to change, categorize people into three groups:

1. ALLIES (100% aligned and ready to roll) — These are your champions. Identify them early and lean on them for support.
2. OPPONENTS (completely disagree) — Understand their objections. Decide if you need to convince them or work around them.
3. UNDECIDED (unsure but willing to give it a shot) — This is the group you can most influence. Focus persuasion efforts here.

Key questions to answer:
- Do you need buy-in from managers outside of the team, or can you handle things internally?
- Do you need a 'come to Jesus' moment with the team, or can changes be made gradually?

### Belief Obsession (Crisis Morale Framework) (Sanchan Saxena)
Shift from data-driven planning to belief-driven planning when data tells you you're dying — rally teams around first-principles beliefs about the future.

How it works: Steps: 1) Acknowledge data is bad and be honest/vulnerable with employees, 2) Identify first-principles beliefs (e.g., 'when COVID is over, people will want to travel'), 3) Find supporting evidence for beliefs (e.g., survey: 99.99% would travel the world if money wasn't a consideration), 4) Use storytelling to make the future crystal clear, 5) Get employees to believe in founder + mission, not just the mission alone. Key insight: 'Gut and intuition is data. It's just not statistically significant yet.'

### Bridges' Transition Model (Ending → Neutral Zone → New Beginning) (Surviving reorgs, tactically and emotionally - Issue 32)
William Bridges' three-stage model for understanding any transition, used here to explain why constant reorgs create 'transition fatigue' when you're stuck in the Neutral Zone

How it works: Three stages of any transition:
1. Ending: The old way of doing things comes to a close. You let go of the previous structure, role, or identity.
2. Neutral Zone: The in-between period where the old is gone but the new isn't fully established. This is where confusion, uncertainty, and 'transition fatigue' live. During constant reorgs, people get stuck here in a prolonged Neutral Zone.
3. New Beginning: The new structure, role, or identity takes hold and you move forward with clarity.

Key insight: During an extended Neutral Zone, bold steps forward are often counterproductive and erroneous. Instead, cultivate good habits that give you clarity, control, and progress.

Recommended reading: 'Transitions' and 'Managing Transitions' by William Bridges.

### Facilitator-First PM Leadership Model (Sachin Monga)
Approach for a head of product joining a founder-led company: start as a facilitator rather than a decision maker

How it works: Steps: (1) First few months — focus solely on bridging the information gap between CEO and teams, (2) Ensure CEO knows what all teams are doing and teams understand the CEO's vision, (3) Help new hires model the founder's thinking by sharing context they missed, (4) Build reps together to establish trust, (5) Establish weekly rhythm — sit down at start and end of week to align on priorities and concerns, (6) Gradually earn decision-making authority through demonstrated alignment.

### Five Factors for Adaptive Resilience During Change (Surviving reorgs, tactically and emotionally - Issue 32)
A framework combining Dr. Michael Cavanagh's Four-Factor Model (Perspective, Purpose, Dialogue, Mindfulness) plus Katherine Hosie's addition of Humor, for building resilience during prolonged organizational change

How it works: Factor 1 - Perspective: Step outside your myopia when stressed. Gain perspective on time ('this too shall pass'), knowledge, and context. Attend conferences, grow your network, read widely including history. Periodically meet recruiters to stay abreast of what keeps you marketable. Think less tree, more forest. Are you even in the right forest?

Factor 2 - Purpose: Define the primary purpose of your role, your team, your organization, and your career. While goals and direction can thrash, purpose creates stability that endures. Regular self-reflection helps you re-orient or double down. Build habits to keep shared purpose front and center (e.g. referring back to it at the start of every team meeting).

Factor 3 - Dialogue: Invest in relationships to create safety, reduce stress, and increase context. Share perspectives and the burden of decisions with others. Look for ways to 'mend the net' — ensure all relationships within and across teams are strong. Specific tactics:
- Set up recurring social dinners with a challenging peer once a quarter
- Initiate bi-weekly triad lunches ('invite a stranger lunches') where you invite one person and ask them to also invite a valued colleague
- New opportunities emerge through relationships

Factor 4 - Mindfulness: Build capacity to dispassionately notice what is happening vs. seeing life through filters, fears, or desires. Start a sustainable meditation practice of 10 minutes per day. Other options: regular exercise, sleep hygiene, journaling. Can be built into how you lead — e.g., meditation at the start of every team meeting. Track these habits to increase agency and motivation.

Factor 5 - Humor: Having a sense of humor about ourselves and life grounds us, makes us more human and connected, and humbles us. Humor helps us relax, become more creative, absorb new ideas and learn better. It grants acceptance that things are as they are, which serves as a springboard for change. Inject humor into your life and work.

Key principle: Think habits, not actions. An action will get you so far, but a smart adaptive habit can fuel you forever. Habits become character, and character is destiny. None of these things done in isolation will do much, but done consistently they will change a lot for you.

### Give Away Your Lego (Cam Adams)
A mental model for scaling startups where employees must hand off tasks they love to new hires in order to take on larger, more complex challenges.

How it works: Used during cultural onboarding. Teaches employees to find joy in building a team, passing on experience, and creating systems rather than holding onto early execution tasks.

### Give Away Your Legos (Molly Graham)
A metaphor for scaling your role by handing off tasks you've mastered to take on new, ambiguous challenges.

How it works: 1. You get a pile of Legos (ambiguity). 2. You build a house and get good at it. 3. Someone new is hired, and you must give them your house to go build a neighborhood. 4. You feel territorial and anxious, but you must let go to scale.

### Give Away Your Legos (Scaling Metaphor) (Essential reading for product builders—part 2)
A mental model for managing the emotional challenge of scaling teams—instead of grabbing back responsibilities from new hires, find bigger problems to solve.

How it works: The Lego Tower Metaphor for Scaling:

1. Early stage: Everyone has tons of Legos (responsibilities), doing 10 jobs, building something important.
2. Growth stage: You add people. New hires start taking some of your Legos.
3. Emotional reaction: People get nervous. First instinct is to grab back the Legos, fight for that part of the tower, or micromanage.
4. The right move: Ignore those instincts. Go find a bigger and better Lego tower to build. There's always a brand-new exciting pile of Legos sitting right next to you.

Key insight: Almost everything about scaling is counterintuitive. Reacting to the emotions of scaling is usually a bad idea.

### Hyper-growth Daily All-Hands Sync (Eric Simons)
A communication cadence used during periods of extreme, unmanageable company growth.

How it works: A daily 1-hour Zoom call with the entire company (15-20 people) at 8:00 AM. Every issue is audited and discussed front-to-back to ensure 0% fidelity loss in communication, allowing high-context individuals to operate autonomously for the rest of the day.

### KPI + Narrative Leadership Model (Tim Holley)
Josh Silverman's approach to driving organizational change by combining a clear KPI with a consistent narrative repeated over time

How it works: Two components: 1) Clarity on goal — a single north star KPI (GMS) that is front and center in every meeting and used as the measuring stick for all launches; 2) Clarity on why — a clear narrative about the strategy and direction, repeated consistently (say it 3 times before people understand, another 3 before they internalize). Marriage of these two creates an 'incredibly powerful combination' for driving change.

### Managing Complex Change (Bangaly Kaba)
A diagnostic tool to identify missing elements in a team's culture or process based on their symptoms.

How it works: 5 required components: Vision, Skills, Incentives, Resources, Action Plan. Missing Vision = Confusion. Missing Incentives = Resistance. Missing Action Plan = False Starts. Missing Skills = Anxiety.

### Motivation Mapping for Change Management (Turning around an underperforming team - Issue 17)
A framework for connecting proposed team changes to individual team members' personal motivations to build intrinsic buy-in.

How it works: For each key team member, identify their primary motivation and frame the change accordingly:

1. If they want to DRIVE IMPACT → Show how the issues you've identified are directly slowing down the team's ability to ship.
2. If they want to be PROMOTED → Show how the issues are keeping team members' careers back.
3. If they want WORK-LIFE BALANCE → Show how the issues are causing everyone unnecessary work.

The approach: Figure out what motivates key members, then connect the changes you want to make with their personal motivations.

### Organizational Kayfabe (Alex Komoroske)
A mental model for understanding how large organizations maintain shared illusions (optimism/status) that diverge from ground truth.

How it works: Recognize that exposing the 'ground truth' abruptly can destroy the org's momentum. Instead, navigate the kayfabe by planting cheap seeds of innovation that don't threaten the official narrative.

### R&D to Product Graduation Process (Ryan J. Salva)
A structured process for transitioning a project from an R&D/moonshot team to an operational product team

How it works: Steps: 1) R&D team experiments freely without monetization or operational constraints; 2) When there's signal with medium confidence that the solution solves a genuine customer problem in a novel way, begin market testing with prototypes; 3) Move researchers temporarily from R&D to seed a new EPD (engineering/product/design) squad; 4) Scale through technical preview (tens of thousands → hundreds of thousands of users); 5) Hire product/engineering team around the researchers; 6) Transition researchers back to R&D only when replacements are in-seat with full skills (not calendar-based); 7) EPD team owns the roadmap going forward. Key principles: researchers must come along temporarily for knowledge transfer; the product team must feel ownership of the roadmap; engineering fundamentals differentiate R&D from product work.

### Recovery Shape Prediction Framework (Leading your company through a pandemic - Issue 20)
Two diagnostic questions to predict whether your business will see a V, U, or L-shaped recovery

How it works: The recovery will not be evenly distributed. Ask two questions:

1. Have many of your customers actually churned, or just gone dormant?
   - More actual churn → more likely U or L-shaped recovery
   - Example: Businesses serving SMBs face real churn because many SMBs don't have cash to survive a month without revenue
   - Dormant customers → more likely V-shaped recovery

2. Has the underlying customer behavior actually changed?
   - True in extreme negative cases (travel, events) and extreme positive cases (online education, food delivery)
   - Permanent behavior change → L-shaped recovery (good or bad)
   - Temporary behavior change → some regression to the mean

Recovery shapes:
- V-shape: Quick bounce back (dormant customers return, behavior unchanged)
- U-shape: Extended trough then recovery (significant churn but behavior eventually returns)
- L-shape (positive): Permanently elevated baseline (behavior shifted in your favor)
- L-shape (negative): Permanently depressed baseline (behavior shifted away from you)

### Swinging the Pendulum (Paul Adams)
A mental model for understanding and navigating organizational over-correction.

How it works: Step 1: Identify an undesirable state. Step 2: Implement a fix. Step 3: Observe if the fix swings too far in the opposite direction (e.g., lacking experts -> hiring only specialists -> losing adaptability). Step 4: Adjust back toward the middle to find the optimal balance.

### The Five-Act Movement Framework (Nancy Duarte)
A structure for leading people through a major change or movement over time.

How it works: Five phases: 1. Dream (verbalize the future), 2. Leap (people commit), 3. Fight (overcoming roadblocks), 4. Climb (the long slog), 5. Arrive. Fuel each phase with targeted speeches, stories, ceremonies, and symbols.

### Top-Down + Bottoms-Up AI Deployment Model (Sherwin Wu V2)
Framework for successfully deploying AI in enterprises that combines executive buy-in with grassroots adoption through internal tiger teams

How it works: Anti-pattern: Pure top-down mandate where execs buy tools and put AI on performance reviews but employees don't know what to do. Success pattern: 1. Top-down: C-suite buys in, purchases tools, provides exec support. 2. Bottoms-up: Find technically-adjacent people (not necessarily engineers) who are excited about AI — 'the Excel wizard on the ops team.' 3. Create a tiger team: Staff full-time internal team to explore capabilities, apply to specific workflows, build best practices. 4. Knowledge share: Run hackathons, seminars, create excitement. 5. Last mile: Let each function figure out their own AI applications because every job is unique.

### Bureaucracy Mirror Doc (This Week #9: Breaking into growth, leading with influence, and (not) stepping on toes 🦶)
A doc or deck format for highlighting organizational inefficiency and proposing fixes

How it works: Purpose: Shine a mirror to the org when small tasks consistently take too long due to bureaucratic friction.

Structure:
1. Collection of examples: Each example should include the task, how small/simple it was, how long it actually took, how many people got involved, and what the ideal timeline should have been.
2. Pattern identification: What's causing these delays? (too many approvers, unclear decision-makers, lack of trust, etc.)
3. Recommendations: What would you change if you were in charge? Propose specific process changes.

How to use: Share with leadership. Two possible outcomes — they agree it's a problem and want to address it, or they explain why things are working as intended. Either way, it moves things forward.

### Cash Runway Assessment Checklist (Leading your company through a pandemic - Issue 20)
Four critical questions to evaluate your financial position during a downturn

How it works: 1. Cash runway: Do you really have as much runway as you think? Could you withstand a few poor quarters? Have you made contingency plans? Where could you trim expenses without fundamentally hurting the business?
2. Fundraising: What would you do if fundraising on attractive terms proves difficult for 1-2 years? Could you turn a challenging situation into an opportunity?
3. Headcount: Can you do more with less and raise productivity?
4. Capital spending: Are your capital spending plans sensible in a more uncertain environment? Could changing circumstances present opportunities to accelerate?

Additional from HBR:
- Produce a rolling report on cash position (weekly or monthly depending on volatility)
- Detail expected near-term payments and receipts
- Estimate midterm cash inflows and outflows
- Consider establishing a centralized cash management system for companywide data and pooling across business units

### Enterprise AI Adoption Playbook (Dan Shipper)
A step-by-step process for getting a company to adopt AI tools effectively.

How it works: 1) The CEO must use AI daily. 2) Send a company-wide 'We are AI-first' memo and explicitly state it was written using AI. 3) Hold weekly meetings to share prompts and use cases. 4) Send a weekly email highlighting usage stats and top internal contributors.

### Incident Response Team Setup Checklist (Leading your company through a pandemic - Issue 20)
Operational checklist for creating a crisis response team within your company

How it works: 1. Assign one person as DRI (Directly Responsible Individual)
2. Give them full capacity to change anything that needs changing
3. Blow up your OKRs. Cut initiatives. Set new priorities, tops-down. Don't just keep the train moving in same direction.
4. Review every single channel (paid ads, automated emails, landing pages) — remove pictures of people in crowds, hanging out in public, etc.
5. Have daily standups with the team, reviewing performance and market changes
6. Move really fast. Fail fast. Win fast. Don't wait. One day today is worth a month later.

Additional from HBR:
- Assemble a small trusted team with enough leeway for rapid tactical decisions
- Avoid overly managing communications
- Use a living digital document (easily updated/withdrawn) instead of multiple approval documents
- Distinguish clearly between facts, hypotheses, and speculations

### Internal AI Adoption Playbook (Brian Balfour)
Actionable steps for executives to force actual AI adoption rather than superficial usage.

How it works: 1. Set hard constraints (e.g., 1/5th headcount size, no new headcount without proving AI can't do it, PRDs require 3 AI prototypes). 2. Categorize employees into catalysts, converts, and anchors. 3. Set hard deadlines for transformation and exit anchors. 4. Measure actual ground-floor adoption. 5. Unblock the slowest part of the system (often IT, legal, or procurement).

### Internal Team Communication During Crisis Checklist (Leading your company through a pandemic - Issue 20)
Guidelines for maintaining team morale and trust through transparent communication during uncertainty

How it works: 1. Be transparent, vulnerable, and open about how worried you are and what you're considering (Elena Verna)
2. Any thoughts in your head — more than likely someone else is thinking about them. Not talking about it creates more uncertainty and stress (Russell Glass)
3. Communicate policies promptly, clearly, and in a balanced manner (HBR)
4. Communicate contextual information and reasoning behind policies so employees can deepen understanding and take initiative in unanticipated situations (HBR)
5. Have your team be together with you — lack of information creates anxiety (Elena Verna)
6. If you are benefiting from the timing, consider doing social good — offer free offerings to schools and non-profits dealing with the crisis (Elena Verna)
7. These are always good brand marketing opportunities, but it's also the right thing to do (Elena Verna)

### Legacy-to-New Platform Onboarding Cycle (Upasna Gautam)
A multi-phase onboarding process for migrating editorial teams from a legacy CMS to a new one

How it works: Phases: 1. Training on the new platform. 2. Working sessions (3-6 per team, 2 hours each) to recreate team-specific workflows. 3. Dialogue and feedback collection. 4. Testing in the new environment. 5. Actual onboarding/go-live. Key principles: Build in buffers (days to months) for unpredictable disruptions. Be ready to compress timeline if buffers aren't needed. Each team's workflows are different, requiring customized sessions.

### Three-Step Layoff Process (Matt Mochary)
A step-by-step guide to conducting a company-wide layoff that minimizes trauma.

How it works: 1. Inner circle sets dollar-amount targets for managers. 2. Morning: Managers deliver news in 1-on-1s and offer to be agents. 3. Afternoon: All-hands meeting to address the stay team's fears (assure them cuts are deep and final). 4. Immediate follow-up: 1-on-1s with every stay team member to process their emotions.

### Ronnie Varghese's 'Do Both' Transformation Strategy (In defense of feature team product managers)
A real-world example of a product leader who ran both feature-team and empowered-team approaches simultaneously for two quarters to prove the empowered model's value with measurable results

How it works: Context: Ronnie joined a feature-factory organization and read Marty Cagan's 'Inspired.' He knew stakeholders wouldn't buy into a concept just because it came from a book.

Approach - The 'Crazy Third Option':
- Option 1: Do the feature factory work (required to maintain credibility)
- Option 2: Attempt the empowered product model (wouldn't get permission)
- Option 3 (chosen): Do BOTH at the same time

Execution:
- For two quarters, the team did two jobs and twice the work—feature factory AND empowered product
- They continuously measured the results of both approaches
- They showed comparative results to stakeholders

Outcome: The empowered model delivered on stakeholders' results agenda, convincing them to eventually buy in

Marty Cagan calls this technique an 'outcome-based roadmap' — a proven transformation approach.

### Statuspage acquisition integration challenges (Tanguy Crusson)
Case study of the difficulties of integrating a startup acquisition into a large company's organizational structure

How it works: Statuspage: ~20-30 person startup acquired by Atlassian. Culture shocks: CEO became product person, GTM person became just marketing. Lost control of daily decisions. Asked for 3-year roadmap (startup had 3-month plans). Team split into organizational silos (product org, engineering org, design org). Hiring a designer required talking to head of design, not CEO. Faced new processes: performance reviews, engineering allocation, revenue forecasts, OKRs. Company said 'keep doing what you're doing' while ground-level teams imposed processes. Product eventually became huge success but painful integration period. Tanguy's lesson: treat acquisitions like hiring, not just buying a business.

### The Sturdy Leader Pilot Metaphor (Dr. Becky Kennedy)
An analogy and script for how to communicate during turbulent company changes.

How it works: Instead of telling people to stop complaining (dismissive) or asking them what to do (weak), use the sturdy approach: 'I hear everyone screaming back there, that makes sense... This turbulence, though it scares you, it doesn't scare me. I know what I'm doing... I'll see you when we land.' Workplace translation: 'I get it, this is a big change... The way you're feeling makes sense. Here's why we're doing it, and I have faith in us to weather this turbulence.'
