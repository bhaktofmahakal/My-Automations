# Founder-Led Sales - Frameworks, Templates & Checklists

*20 artifacts extracted from Lenny's Podcast and Newsletter*

## Frameworks

### Amplitude's 'Buy This Month' Sales Heuristic (How to identify your ideal customer profile (ICP))
A tactical sales approach for early-stage startups: only focus on prospects who can buy this month, quote high, and negotiate down for speed.

How it works: Rules:
1. Only focus on people who can buy THIS MONTH
2. If someone says the project is a few months out, don't talk to them
3. When they ask for a price, quote a really high price (e.g. $100,000/year)
4. When they say 'I can only pay $20K,' respond: 'Cool. Can you do it this month? If you can, then let's go.'
5. Prioritize your time around getting customers to pay something, as soon as possible

### B2B Startup Maturity Stages / Founder-led Selling Stages (Pete Kazanjy)
A sequential progression model for B2B startups: (1) Validate the problem, (2) Build MVP, (3) Founder sells to 20-30 customers, (4) Hire 2 pioneer sellers and get them to succeed, (5) Scale to 4, then 8, then 16 sellers. Skipping stages leads to failure.

How it works: Stage 1: Customer development/problem validation. Stage 2: Build minimum feature set. Stage 3: Founder sells — achieve 15-25% win rate over 50-100 at-bats. Stage 4: Hire 2 pioneer sellers, transfer sales motion from founder's brain to docs/slides/scripts. Stage 5: Get both sellers to ~20% win rate. Stage 6: Hire a sales manager/leader. Stage 7: Scale to 4, 8, 16 sellers. Each stage must be completed before moving to the next.

### Consultant with a Predilection (Pete Kazanjy)
A reframe of the seller's role — you're a consultant who happens to represent a particular solution, not someone trying to convince people who don't need what you have.

How it works: Anti-pattern: 'Sell ice to an Eskimo' — if you're selling to someone who doesn't need it, you're doing it wrong. Correct approach: 1. Find people with high proclivity for your solution. 2. Through discovery questions, reveal they have a high-magnitude problem. 3. Show them it costs them money/pain. 4. Reveal there's a better way. 5. Introduce yourself as representative of that solution. Test: Would you tell a prospect 'don't buy this' if they're not ICP? (Pete's example: telling a 1-person team not to buy Atrium).

### Founder's Sell-First Enterprise Sales Validation (Varun Mohan)
Before hiring a VP of Sales or sales team, the founder must personally sell the product to understand the scaling path. If the founder can't sell incrementally, they can't hand off the process.

How it works: Steps: 1) Founder sells the product personally. 2) If founder finds it hard to sell incrementally, DON'T hire sales—you can't scale what you don't understand. 3) When inbound interest validates demand and founder understands the motion (e.g., running dozens of concurrent pilots), THEN hire VP of Sales. 4) Scale sales team rapidly. Codeium example: Varun + team ran tens of concurrent enterprise pilots, validated the motion, then hired VP of Sales by end of 2023, scaling to 80+ go-to-market people.

### Four Signs It's Time to Hire a Full-Time Salesperson (Hiring your early team)
Qualitative and quantitative signals that indicate a founder should transition from founder-led sales to a dedicated salesperson

How it works: Sign 1: You can sell consistently — You have a pitch that works and a repeatable process you could teach someone else. (Eric Glyman, Ramp; Mathilde Collin, Front with 200 customers)

Sign 2: It gets boring and you're not learning — Sales calls become repeatable: get on, say your script, turn your brain off, send DocuSign. (Christina Cacioppo, Vanta; Rujul Zaparde, Zip)

Sign 3: You're overwhelmed with inbound — More deal flow than you can handle. (Barry McCardel, Hex)

Sign 4: You hit $300-500k ARR — The sweet spot where most founders brought on a salesperson.

Data points: Zip $200k ARR (4th employee), Hex $250k (8th), Amplitude ~$300k (2nd), Sprig $300k + $700k pipeline, Census mid-six figures (4th), Vanta $500k (9th), Stytch low six figures (8th), Retool $2-3M, Linear couple million, Notion $10-15M, Canva ~$120M.

Takeaway: Past $200k ARR start looking; before $1M you should probably have someone.

### Internal Pessimism / External Optimism (Sahil Mansuri)
Great salespeople (and CEOs) balance internal pessimism—constantly looking for disqualifying signals—with external optimism to avoid wasting time on deals that won't close

How it works: Good salespeople: Get misled by customers who say they want to buy, waste time on deals that never close ('happy ears'). Great salespeople: Internally pessimistic—actively trying to disqualify, looking for signals the prospect can't or won't buy. Externally optimistic—still positively engaging and selling. Application to fundraising: Give VCs every out to not continue the conversation. If they still want to talk after that, they're serious. If a VC says one or two good things, don't assume they'll invest—test harder.

### Runs on My Local → Runs in the Cloud (Pete Kazanjy)
A metaphor for the transition from founder-led sales to a scalable sales team — first prove it works reliably with you, then see if it reproduces with hired sellers.

How it works: Step 1: Founder sells, achieves 15-25% win rate over 50-100 at-bats ('runs on local'). Step 2: Package the sales motion into slides, scripts, discovery questions, email templates, demo scripts. Step 3: Hire 2 sellers and transfer the packaged motion to them ('deploy to cloud'). Step 4: If it breaks, debug — is it a coaching issue, a materials issue, or a hiring issue? Step 5: Once 2 sellers succeed, scale to 4, then 8.

### The Sales Learning Curve (Mark Leslie) (Hiring your early team)
Framework from HBR article explaining how the salesperson's job differs across three phases of startup maturity

How it works: Referenced article: Mark Leslie's 'The Sales Learning Curve' in Harvard Business Review. Key insight as applied by Gusto: In the beginning phase, your early salesperson's job is NOT to close-close-close and create FOMO. Instead, they should be a product person generating insights—experimenting with approaches, identifying opportunities, and informing the product roadmap. The job evolves through three phases of company maturity, from insight generation to repeatable sales.

### Turbo Rapport (Pete Kazanjy)
A practice method for building the rapid rapport-building skill essential to sales — practice by quickly befriending strangers in everyday situations (baristas, flight attendants, people at crosswalks).

How it works: Practice targets: people who are shields-up (bartenders, flight attendants, baristas). Goal: break down defenses quickly and become friendly within seconds. Everyday version: make eye contact with everyone walking down the street, strike up conversations at Starbucks/crosswalks. Rules: compliment something specific (shirt, shoes), don't use weather (lazy). Application: in sales, you have 90 seconds to form trust and get someone to be honest with you.

## Checklists

### Founder-Led Sales Readiness Checklist (Raaz Herzberg)
Principles for when founders should be selling themselves versus hiring salespeople

How it works: 1. Founders must close deals end-to-end themselves first (contracts and all). 2. Reach a couple million ARR before hiring first salesperson. 3. If the core group can't sell it, an outside hire won't be able to either. 4. Don't hire to solve a problem you haven't solved yourself at least once. 5. Having founder-closed deals gives first sales hires confidence ('We sold a couple million, so you can too'). 6. Same applies to product marketing and other functions — do it yourself first before hiring.

### Takeaways on Hiring Your First Full-Time Salesperson (Hiring your early team)
Summary checklist for the founder-led sales to first sales hire transition

How it works: 1. Start with founder-led sales — do the selling yourself until you can sell consistently, it gets too boring, or you're overwhelmed with demand.
2. Consider bringing on a sales consultant/coach (normally a former VP of Sales) to help you during the early period.
3. Once you hit $300-500k ARR, it's probably time to bring on a full-time salesperson.
4. Your first hire should probably be a hungry senior AE. If not, they should act like one: dialing, gathering insights to inform the product/GTM, and closing deals themselves.

### The $1 Invoice Test (Jeff Weinstein)
A tactic to help founders cross the psychological barrier of charging customers.

How it works: Have the founder sign up for an invoicing service and immediately send a $1 invoice to a friend/advisor. Gets the logo set up, puts $1 in the account, and removes the friction of the 'first charge'.

### Warm Intro via Text Message Technique (Sahil Mansuri)
A tactical process for getting and converting warm introductions using text messages instead of email to dramatically increase response and meeting rates

How it works: Steps: 1) Ask existing customer/contact for a specific name of someone who has the same problem. 2) Don't just get the name—ask 'Can you make me an e-intro?' 3) Even better: 'Can you connect me over text?' 4) Get a text intro with the prospect (not email—'email is where deals go to die'). 5) Do NOT remove the introducer from the text thread. 6) Keep introducer on the thread for the first 10-20 messages. 7) This holds the prospect's feet to the fire to actually show up. 8) If prospect goes dark, keep pinging the group thread every 1-2 weeks. 9) Social pressure from introducer ('you're making me look bad') eventually converts. Example: Sahil kept pinging a group text for 9 weeks until the prospect finally engaged and became a customer.

## Examples

### GPT-4 Sales Prospecting Script (Eric Ries)
An example of using AI for high-volume, highly personalized outbound sales.

How it works: A founder downloaded a list of 10,000 Shopify stores with metadata and used a Python script to have GPT-4 write highly creative, customized pitch emails for each store based on their specific tone and products, resulting in very high engagement.

### Hustle Distribution Tactics from Famous Founders (Finding your distribution advantage)
Collection of real guerrilla distribution tactics used by founders of billion-dollar companies in their early days.

How it works: 1. Tony Xu (DoorDash) — Personally delivered food in the early days
2. Jack Dorsey (Square) — Walked from retailer to retailer pitching the Square dongle to owners
3. Travis Kalanick (Uber) — Handed out Uber coupon codes at Caltrain stations
4. Ben Silbermann (Pinterest) — Went to Apple stores and changed their homepage browsers to Pinterest.com
5. Netflix — Made a deal with DVD manufacturers to insert flyers into DVD player boxes
6. Kayak — Made a deal with AOL to use Kayak for flight search
7. PayPal — Partnered with eBay to handle payments

### Jellyfish's First 20 Customers Email (Jen Abel)
The exact email structure Jen used to get her first 20 customers.

How it works: Opening line: 'Zero to one sales talent doesn't exist. That's why I want to have a conversation with you.' Followed by relevancy: 'I noticed you're looking to target X, Y, and Z based off of your website.' Closing: 'Our belief at Jellyfish is zero to one sales talent doesn't exist. That's why we built this model.'

### Sales Consultant Bridge Strategy (Gong and Amplitude) (Hiring your early team)
How two companies used part-time sales consultants to bridge the gap between founder-led sales and a full-time sales hire

How it works: Gong: Hired a part-time consultant who had been VP of Sales at various companies. He was VP-level but still willing to dial. Closed roughly half of their first 12 customers. This solved the IC-vs-VP dilemma: an IC doesn't know what to do; a VP wants to hire a team when there's no playbook. After seeing things work, they started hiring full-time salespeople—the first 2-3 are still with the company years later.

Amplitude: Hired Mitch Morando as a sales coach who met with founders once a week. He helped sort the wheat from the chaff in existing customers and pipeline very quickly. After that, the business really started taking off. Key insight: 'You don't want to learn sales out of a book. You've got to go through the motions and get the reps. The best salespeople learn by getting someone really good at sales to coach them regularly.'

### The Collison Install (Dalton Caldwell)
A tactic for closing early enterprise sales and ensuring product implementation, pioneered by the founders of Stripe.

How it works: When a customer agrees to use your product, do not wait for them to implement it. Say 'I'm in the neighborhood, I'll drop by.' Go to their office, ask for their keyboard, and physically install the software into their codebase yourself.

### Zoom's Pre-Product Sales and Deliberate Team Building (How today's fastest-growing B2B startups turned their early users into paying customers)
How Zoom sold before the product was ready and deliberately delayed marketing

How it works: First deal: Stanford Continuing Studies Group, closed before the solution was ready. Team composition: No sales team, no marketing team, just engineers. Strategy: Before reaching $1M ARR, deliberately did not create a marketing team — only a sales team. After founding team closed several deals, hired a VP of Sales who then hired sales reps. Lead generation primarily came from organic leads — happy customers referring other customers, plus freemium product generating qualifying leads.

## Tools

### Sales Learning Resource List (SEO keywords, career ladders, backlog tools, copywriting, OnlyFans, AMA with Pete Kazanjy and much more)
Pete Kazanjy's recommended books and communities for learning sales as a founder or non-sales professional.

How it works: Books: 1) Founding Sales by Pete Kazanjy (foundingsales.com) — specifically for founders learning to sell. 2) Cracking the Sales Management Code — sales management systems. 3) The Transparency Sale — modern honest selling. 4) The Challenger Sale — consultative/insight-selling methodology. 5) To Sell Is Human by Daniel Pink — reframing sales for everyone. 6) Persuasion (Influence) by Robert Cialdini — psychology of persuasion. Communities: Modern Sales (modernsaleshq.com) — 20k member sales operations and leadership community run by Atrium. Peer education is key since there's no formal 'sales degree.'
