## David Singleton

**Insight:** Effective product reviews rely on early and frequent exposure to real users to validate that the solution meets a high bar of craftsmanship.

**Tactical advice:**
- Demonstrate product prototypes to users frequently to ensure the team is solving the right problems.
- Focus reviews on the 'meticulousness' of the craft and how it serves the end user's needs.
- Include cross-functional perspectives early in the development cycle to identify friction points before they reach production.

## Lenny Rachitsky

> "One thing I encourage for both is to first present the “option space”—it’s really powerful to have a framework that maps all possible solutions or problems and use that as a device to discuss high-level tradeoffs or philosophical differences."

**Insight:** High-quality product decisions are best made by evaluating an entire framework of options rather than debating a single proposed solution in isolation.

**Tactical advice:**
- Present a framework mapping all possible solutions to surface philosophical differences.
- Use alignment widgets or sentiment voting to gauge the room's reaction to specific directions.
- Shift from text-heavy docs to visual storytelling to facilitate more interactive conversations.

*Source: [How Figma builds product](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/how-figma-builds-product)*

> "Product review meetings happen every Tuesday and Thursday for a total of two hours. These two hours are divided into 20-minute slots that product teams sign up to present. The agenda of these meetings is product teams presenting their proposed product changes for the first 5 to 10 minutes and then reviewers asking questions and giving feedback for the rest of the time."

**Insight:** Establishing a regular, transparent review cadence with the CEO and cross-functional leads creates a shared understanding of product quality and accelerates decision-making.

**Tactical advice:**
- Schedule recurring, short (20-minute) slots for product reviews to prevent feedback bottlenecks and keep teams moving.
- Standardize spec templates and presentation formats to ensure reviews focus on decision-making rather than logistics.
- Open review meetings to the entire company to help new PMs and designers understand the company's product philosophy and logic.

*Source: [How Duolingo builds product](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/how-duolingo-builds-product)*

> "Product reviews can focus on different stages of the product development lifecycle: P-Strat: long-term strategy and vision; P0: the opportunity and problem that we want to pursue; P1: the proposed solution; P2: what we launched and how it’s performing."

**Insight:** Structuring product reviews into distinct stages based on the development lifecycle ensures alignment on the problem before teams commit to a solution.

**Tactical advice:**
- Define specific templates for each review stage to standardize the information teams must provide.
- Hold P0 reviews early to ensure leadership agreement on the opportunity and problem space.
- Review launched initiatives (P2) to evaluate performance and capture learnings for future iterations.

*Source: [How Miro builds product](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/how-miro-builds-product)*

> "One of the product leaders came up with the 'Mona Lisa principle'—simply put, everything we ship should be like a Mona Lisa painting, something we’d be proud of putting our name on. . . Every month, they review everything that was shipped and say what is high-quality or not."

**Insight:** Defining quality through simple metaphors and following up with regular retrospective reviews helps teams operationalize a high bar for excellence.

**Tactical advice:**
- Adopt a simple quality mantra, like the 'Mona Lisa principle,' to build intuition across the team.
- Conduct monthly design reviews of shipped features to calibrate the quality bar and provide feedback.
- Share real-world examples of high quality to provide teams with concrete benchmarks for success.

*Source: [How Miro builds product](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/how-miro-builds-product)*

> "It’s actually been really good practice for the PM team, because most of these reviews come with a short video from the PM explaining what the product or project is. I think it’s great practice for PMs to have to say, very concisely, 'What is this thing? Why is it valuable? How does it work?'"

**Insight:** Standardizing the project lifecycle into clear review phases and requiring async video updates improves PM storytelling and ensures cross-functional alignment.

**Tactical advice:**
- Define distinct review phases such as Proposal, Prototype, Build, Release, and Results.
- Require PMs to submit short videos for project reviews to sharpen their value proposition messaging.
- Establish a two-tier review system (OK1 and OK2) involving functional directors and senior leadership.

> "The goal of a product review isn’t to “sell” the leadership team on a new investment but to detail customer requirements, align on relative business priorities, and provide a clear direction of how to address these challenges."

**Insight:** Structure product reviews as alignment sessions rather than sales pitches to ensure consistent decision-making across the organization.

**Tactical advice:**
- Allow any PM to queue a review in the schedule backlog for feedback.
- Involve co-founders and top engineering leadership directly in the review discussions.
- Focus the meeting on detailing customer requirements and relative business priorities.

*Source: [How Snowflake builds product](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/how-snowflake-builds-product)*

> "We don’t have formal product or design reviews. It’s more ad hoc and iterative, which enables us to move faster. For example, our designers share an early design in a project-related Slack group and get informal asynchronous feedback from many different people that way."

**Insight:** Replacing formal meetings with continuous, asynchronous feedback loops and internal testing accelerates development cycles.

**Tactical advice:**
- Use dedicated Slack groups for informal and asynchronous design feedback.
- Implement a robust feature-flag system to push features to internal testing immediately.
- Leverage a beta testing program with trusted customers for early product feedback.

*Source: [How Linear builds product](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/how-linear-builds-product)*

> "We are relatively extreme in letting the teams autonomously drive their own agendas. That is, once a pod has been assigned an area of responsibility, a great outcome is that they come up with the respective product design. So the leadership team is hardly involved in the detailed design or the iterations with customers along the way."

**Insight:** Empowered teams should own their design process end-to-end, using leadership primarily for cross-group alignment rather than detailed feature approvals.

**Tactical advice:**
- Allow pods to pre-launch features and iterate based on customer feedback without requiring top-down sign-off.
- Hold quarterly alignment meetings with product group leaders to identify and resolve platform-wide overlaps.
- Escalate decision-making to leadership only when conflicts arise between competing group timelines or resource needs.

> "At Ramp, we build in the open and empower teams as much as possible to make the decisions in order to move quickly. What this means in practice is that every spec, design, decision, progress, and status is published in project-specific Slack channels, and anyone is invited to read and opine."

**Insight:** Replace slow approval processes with a 'farm for dissent' model that uses transparent communication and lightweight, non-blocking jam sessions to maintain velocity.

**Tactical advice:**

- Publish all specs and designs in open Slack channels to eliminate gatekeepers and allow for asynchronous feedback.
- Empower PMs to skip formal review meetings for any project that does not have a 'big impact' on core product goals.

## Tomer Cohen

> "I push a lot for what is actually your opinion, what is your potentially controversial opinion and the best principles have teeth. So saying that we should build a simple product for me is useless. Who doesn't want to build a simple product?"

**Insight:** Product reviews should be rigorous deep-dives into specific problems and principled opinions, rather than high-level status updates.

**Tactical advice:**
- Spend significant time clarifying the exact, nuanced problem statement before discussing any solutions.
- Require team members to present opinions that involve clear trade-offs and "teeth."
- Distinguish between misunderstandings and disagreements to avoid wasting time in circular arguments.

*Source: [Why LinkedIn is turning PMs into AI-powered "full stack builders” | Tomer Cohen (LinkedIn CPO)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=R-zCfLQD_84) @ 00:06:46*
