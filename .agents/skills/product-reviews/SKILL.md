# Effective Product Reviews

Run rigorous alignment sessions that raise the quality bar and accelerate decision-making.

Help the user with effective product reviews using insights from 12 guests and posts across Lenny's Podcast and Newsletter.

## How to Help

1. **Assess review culture** - Evaluate current review formats and identify bottlenecks or gaps in quality standards.
2. **Select a framework** - Recommend the appropriate review stage or template based on the project's complexity and lifecycle.
3. **Draft review materials** - Assist in framing problem statements, mapping the option space, and preparing pre-read documents.
4. **Facilitate the session** - Provide techniques for gauging sentiment, managing dissent, and capturing clear decisions.

## Core Principles

### Evaluate Frameworks, Not Solutions
From "How Figma builds product": "One thing I encourage for both is to first present the “option space”—it’s really powerful to have a framework that maps all possible solutions or problems and use that as a device to discuss high-level tradeoffs or philosophical differences."

High-quality decisions come from mapping the entire field of possible solutions rather than debating a single proposal in isolation.

### Establish a Transparent Cadence
From "How Duolingo builds product": "Product review meetings happen every Tuesday and Thursday for a total of two hours. These two hours are divided into 20-minute slots that product teams sign up to present. The agenda of these meetings is product teams presenting their proposed product changes for the first 5 to 10 minutes and then reviewers asking questions and giving feedback for the rest of the time."

Recurring, short review slots prevent feedback bottlenecks and create a shared understanding of what constitutes quality across the organization.

### Separate Problem from Solution
From "How Miro builds product": "Product reviews can focus on different stages of the product development lifecycle: P-Strat: long-term strategy and vision; P0: the opportunity and problem that we want to pursue; P1: the proposed solution; P2: what we launched and how it’s performing."

Structure reviews into distinct stages to ensure leadership agrees on the problem and the strategic direction before teams commit to high-fidelity design work.

### Operationalize Quality Mantras
From "How Miro builds product": "One of the product leaders came up with the 'Mona Lisa principle'—simply put, everything we ship should be like a Mona Lisa painting, something we’d be proud of putting our name on. . . Every month, they review everything that was shipped and say what is high-quality or not."

Use simple metaphors or principles to build intuition across the team, helping everyone understand the bar for excellence without constant oversight.

### Alignment Over Sales Pitches
From "How Snowflake builds product": "The goal of a product review isn’t to “sell” the leadership team on a new investment but to detail customer requirements, align on relative business priorities, and provide a clear direction of how to address these challenges."

Format reviews as collaborative sessions focused on consistent decision-making rather than internal marketing or selling an idea to leadership.

### Focus on Rigorous Problem Clarification
Tomer Cohen: "I push a lot for what is actually your opinion, what is your potentially controversial opinion and the best principles have teeth. So saying that we should build a simple product for me is useless. Who doesn't want to build a simple product?"

Reviews should be deep-dives into specific problems and principled opinions, avoiding the trap of high-level status updates that provide no strategic value.

## Questions to Help Users

- "What stage of the product development lifecycle is this project currently in?"
- "Have you mapped out the full range of possible solutions or are you presenting a single path?"
- "What is the specific, nuanced problem statement you are trying to solve?"
- "How does this project rank on the uncertainty versus impact matrix?"
- "Are there dissenting opinions within the team that should be surfaced during this review?"
- "What are the principled opinions that the team is bringing to this discussion?"

## Common Mistakes to Flag

- **Treating reviews as status updates** - This wastes leadership time and fails to address strategic blockers or quality issues.
- **Reviewing too late in the process** - Teams become wedded to a solution, making leadership feedback feel like a demoralizing roadblock rather than guidance.
- **Seeking consensus instead of alignment** - Waiting for everyone to agree slows down development and often results in watered-down product decisions.
- **Debating solutions before agreeing on the problem** - This leads to circular arguments where stakeholders are optimizing for different goals.
