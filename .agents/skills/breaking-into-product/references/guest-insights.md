## Anneka Gupta

**Insight:** The most reliable way to transition into product management is through internal mobility, leveraging established trust and product-adjacent experience.

**Tactical advice:**
- Volunteer for tasks that support the product team in your current role to build a relevant track record and internal trust.
- Leverage your expertise in a product-adjacent field to provide valuable data or insights to current PMs.
- Identify user pain points and propose data-backed solutions to demonstrate your product thinking to leadership.

*Source: [Becoming more strategic, navigating difficult colleagues, harnessing founder mode, and more | Anneka Gupta (Chief Product Officer at Rubrik)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=E3dUveqt9Bw) @ 00:51:41*

## Annie Pearl

> "I think there's really two paths. I think one is more formal in nature. There are associate product manager programs out there and many scaled companies, Google, Meta. All have APM programs that you can formally apply to."

**Insight:** Breaking into product management is most effectively achieved through internal transfers, formal APM programs, or by joining early-stage startups where cross-functional work is expected.

**Tactical advice:**
- Apply to formal Associate Product Manager (APM) programs at scaled or mid-stage companies.
- Partner closely with PMs internally to shadow their work and take on product-related tasks.
- Search for junior PM roles on internal job boards if you are already in an adjacent function like sales engineering or customer support.

*Source: [Behind the scenes of Calendly’s rapid growth | Annie Pearl (CPO)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-tUIGpgmsZw) @ 00:06:25*

## Christopher Miller

> "I think the pedigree of product manager at HubSpot at that time was also a bit different. There were folks who maybe started their time at HubSpot in support, and so intimately familiar with the product and with customers. Some of these people had closed thousands of support tickets and my background was a bit different."

**Insight:** Transitioning into product management is often most successful when you leverage existing deep expertise in customer support or internal business operations.

**Tactical advice:**
- Develop deep empathy for users by handling a high volume of support tickets or customer calls.
- Seek out high-visibility problems the business isn't explicitly asking people to solve.
- Proactively join strategic conversations even if you do not yet have the official title.

*Source: [Relentless curiosity, radical accountability, and HubSpot’s winning growth formula | Christopher Miller (VP of Product, Growth and AI)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SzKKJwuRFZI) @ 00:17:22*

## Deb Liu

> "I think when you have passion around a product or passion around a company or around a business model or around something, it shows. And so it's not necessarily faking the enthusiasm or faking the idea that you want to work there, but you don't have to know how to write this spec or PRD or briefings or anything like that. You don't know how to do customer research or do data analytics or read reports, but instead show your passion around the product itself, around the use case, around the customer."

**Insight:** Demonstrating a genuine obsession with solving a user's problem is more effective than relying on formal credentials when breaking into product management.

**Tactical advice:**
- Lean into your passion for the product and the company during interviews to compensate for a lack of formal PM training.
- Demonstrate your potential by providing rich feedback on product use cases and suggesting new features based on your experience as a user.
- Focus on falling in love with the problem and the customer's needs rather than trying to master PM frameworks immediately.

*Source: How to own your career growth and become a powerful product leader | Deb Liu, Ancestry (ex-Facebook, PayPal) @ 00:07:14*

## Kevin Yien

## Lenny Rachitsky

> "I switched from engineering to product about ten years into my engineering career, and looking back it was one of the best decisions of my life. Though I loved programming, frankly, I was a mediocre engineer. I got my work done, but I knew I was never going to be an amazing engineer."

**Insight:** Transitioning from engineering to product management is often the right move if you find yourself naturally gravitating toward coordination and strategy despite a baseline love for programming.

**Tactical advice:**
- Evaluate whether you are already naturally performing and enjoying PM-related tasks like talking to stakeholders and customers.
- Identify if you feel like a mediocre engineer who isn't motivated to put in the time to become significantly better.
- Accept that your technical coding skills will atrophy as you shift your focus toward developing essential soft skills.

*Source: [Should I become a product manager](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/should-i-become-a-product-manager)*

- Drive shipping by making tradeoff decisions, helping estimate timelines, and unblocking the team's progress.

**Insight:** Analytical thinking interviews evaluate your thought process and ability to structure a complex product problem rather than just finding a single correct answer.

**Tactical advice:**
- Spend the first minute making scope-narrowing assumptions to avoid getting overwhelmed.
- Explicitly share your game plan at the start of the interview to align with the interviewer's expectations.
- Pause between sections to confirm the interviewer is following your logic before moving to the next framework component.

## Naomi Gleit

> "I ended up going, sort of the analogy, I went to the second floor most days after work, asked if there were any projects that I could help out with. It was very early days. There was always more to do than people to do it. And so eventually I picked up a few projects, helping with program management, giving my product feedback, and by the time that I actually applied formally to be a product manager, I had been doing the job voluntarily, almost informally for a few months."

**Insight:** To transition into a product management role without a traditional background, secure any position at a high-growth company and prove your capabilities by performing PM tasks voluntarily before formally applying.

**Tactical advice:**
- Prioritize getting a 'seat on the rocket ship' by accepting any available non-technical role at a fast-growing company.
- Spend time in the product department after your official work hours to help with program management and provide feedback.
- Perform the responsibilities of your target role informally for several months to build a track record before requesting a formal title change.

*Source: [Meta’s Head of Product (and 29th employee) on working with Mark Zuckerberg, early growth tactics, why PMs are like conductors, and more | Naomi Gleit](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=sTYuKgzZoL8) @ 00:06:54*
