# Breaking Into Product Management - Frameworks, Templates & Checklists

*11 artifacts extracted from Lenny's Podcast and Newsletter*

## Frameworks

### Broad Then Deep (Preparing for a PM interview)
A general-purpose framework for answering any PM interview question by first going broad and then drilling into depth.

How it works: Used for: Any question
Approach: Start with a broad exploration of the space, then go deep on the most important area
Reference: https://www.tryexponent.com/courses/general-interview/broad-then-deep

### Four Paths into Product Management (How to get into product management)
A framework of the four most common routes people take to get their first PM role, each with prerequisites and key success factors

How it works: Path 1: Internal transition at a large company
- Prerequisites: (1) Internal transfer process exists, (2) Chance to demonstrate PM skills, (3) Internal PM champion (generally your new manager)
- If two of three exist, find a way to make the third happen
- Generally the easiest and quickest route

Path 2: Junior PM role at a large company
- Prerequisites: Company has APM or internship programs
- Common route for MBA graduates (MBA not required)
- Alternatives: Product School, General Assembly, PMHQ community
- Key: Clearly demonstrate you are smart, driven, and have raw strengths in core PM skills
- Likely the most common route

Path 3: Joining a startup with a burning need
- Prerequisites: Connections with startup founders, showing hustle, delivering success
- Sources: HN Jobs, AngelList
- Key: Growth mindset since you'll learn on the fly
- Can start as founding PM or transition into it
- Example: Airbnb's original head of product got in this way

Path 4: Starting your own company
- Most work-intensive path, rarely planned
- CEOs often become PMs after acquisition
- Founders can take on PM role as company grows
- This is how Lenny personally got into PM
- Not recommended as primary route to becoming a PM

### Internal Transition Prerequisites (Three Things That Must Align) (How to get into product management)
A simple three-part checklist for determining if an internal PM transition is viable

How it works: For an internal transition to PM to work, three things must align:
1. An internal transfer process of some kind exists at your company
2. You have had a chance to demonstrate PM skills (strategy, execution, communication, etc.)
3. You have an internal PM champion for your transition (generally your new manager)

Rule: If two of three exist, find a way to make the third happen. Otherwise, pursue one of the other paths.

## Templates

### Assumption-Setting Template for Product Sense Interviews (The definitive guide to mastering product sense interviews)
A fill-in-the-blank template for the 2-4 assumptions candidates should state at the start of any product sense interview

How it works: Three categories of assumptions to state: (1) Role and context: 'I'll assume I'm the PM for [product/feature] at [company], building within [company]'s existing product ecosystem.' (2) Geographic focus: 'I'll focus on the [U.S./specific] market because [reason], and we can likely scale our solution globally in the future.' (3) Platform and constraints: 'For distribution advantages and accelerated impact, I'll plan to design a solution within [company's existing platform/ecosystem] rather than as a standalone product.' Optional additions: Scope narrowing (e.g., 'I'll focus on [specific user type] rather than [other type]'), format constraints (e.g., 'I'll focus on audio for this exercise'), audience constraints (e.g., 'I'd like to limit to existing subscribers').

## Checklists

### Estimation Question Approach (The definitive guide to mastering analytical thinking interviews)
Step-by-step method for answering estimation/ballpark questions in PM interviews

How it works: Steps:
1. **Restate the question** to ensure understanding of what you're estimating
2. **Break down the question** by creating a logical formula isolating all necessary components
3. **Plug in reasonable assumptions** using personal experience and common knowledge as reference points; use round numbers in the right order of magnitude
4. **Include all critical components** — missing one makes estimates fundamentally flawed
5. **Calculate and sanity-check** your response
6. **Ask the interviewer** if the final estimate seems reasonable
7. **Prepare for follow-up questions** to track your thinking

### PM Role Fit Self-Assessment (How to get into product management)
Two contrasting lists to help someone honestly assess whether the PM role aligns with their motivations and personality

How it works: BECOME a product manager if you are fulfilled by:
1. Solving people's problems (both your users' and your team's)
2. Driving business growth
3. Working closely with a variety of people
4. Developing a strategy
5. Getting shit done
6. Leading a team (through influence, not authority)
7. Communicating often and broadly
8. Making decisions
9. Creating amazing experiences for people
10. Being organized, detail oriented, and prepared

DO NOT become a product manager if you are primarily fulfilled by:
1. Appreciation
2. Having your way
3. Being left alone
4. Always being right
5. Designing or building things yourself
6. Everyone liking you
7. Flow states
8. Avoiding meetings
9. Avoiding email
10. Avoiding people
