## Lenny Rachitsky

> "As the company starts to scale, relying purely on self-serve often results in an asymptotic flattening of the growth curve, resulting in linear or worse, declining growth."

**Insight:** Product-led companies must eventually add human sales to navigate organizational sign-offs and capture larger deal sizes that self-serve motions cannot reach.

**Tactical advice:**
- Introduce a 'sales assist' team to help close and expand larger accounts that enter via your product-led motion.
- Add humans to the GTM motion once you move into Mid-market and Enterprise segments to handle education and deal-winning.
- Layer on sales before the self-serve growth curve begins to flatten to maintain venture-scale momentum.

*Source: [GTM motions of 30 B2B SaaS companies](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/gtm-motions-of-30-b2b-saas-companies)*

> "The question is not either or but both and a matter of sequencing. PLG is perfect for lowering the barrier for more people to try the product and broaden the reach."

**Insight:** Adding PLG to a sales-led company is not about replacing the sales team but about creating a complementary funnel that lowers entry barriers and expands market reach.

**Tactical advice:**
- Sequence the motion so PLG serves as the wide entry point while sales targets high-value accounts.
- Commit to a long-term roadmap of a year or more rather than expecting instant results from a trial.
- Ensure the organization understands that PLG and sales-led motions serve different segments of the funnel.

*Source: [Summary: The ultimate guide to adding a PLG motion | Hila Qu (Reforge, GitLab)](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/summary-the-ultimate-guide-to-adding-a-plg-motion--hila-qu-reforge-gitlab)*

> "The crux of this article is that waiting too long to add sales-involvement often leads to a large opportunity cost. Many successful self-serve applications saw their market position usurped by competitors who adopted a sales-assisted motion and effectively firewalled the self-serve-only products out of lucrative enterprise segments (e.g. Dropbox)."

**Insight:** Delaying the addition of sales to a self-serve product can allow competitors to capture high-value enterprise segments and lock you out of the market.

**Tactical advice:**
- Identify if human intervention is needed to smooth over edge cases or communicate complicated concepts during onboarding.
- Use account managers to consolidate multiple individual user "pods" within a large organization into a single enterprise contract.
- Calculate the value of a "sales assist" by determining if human outreach significantly increases conversion probability for stalled users.

> "Imagine a situation where the average user of your software ends up spending $5,000 a year. And further imagine you know that engaging with a hung, unactivated user doubles the probability they convert to a customer from 10% to 20%. Lastly, you know that the time cost of doing so is a couple of hours, by a staff resource that costs $50 an hour. With those inputs, we’re essentially saying “I can spend $100 in human labor to capture $500 in expected conversion upside.”"

**Insight:** Sales teams are economically viable for self-serve products only when the human labor cost required to convert a user is substantially lower than the resulting revenue uplift.

**Tactical advice:**
- Model potential revenue gains by comparing current conversion rates to projected rates with human assistance.
- Compare the hourly cost of a sales resource against the expected dollar value of the conversion upside they generate.
- Differentiate between users whose potential lifetime value justifies human outreach and those who should remain purely self-serve.
