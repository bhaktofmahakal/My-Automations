## Austin Hay

> "That's how you actually have a lot of velocity is thinking about not just build versus buy. It's build and buy now. So, you buy the tool to get 90% of the way there and then you build the cool thing on top with the other 10%. And so that architecting decision usually falls on this person."

**Insight:** High-velocity teams avoid the binary choice of building or buying by using SaaS for core functionality and building proprietary layers for the remaining specialized needs.

**Tactical advice:**
- Buy third-party solutions for foundational needs like data routing and analytics to avoid 'reinventing the wheel.'
- Deploy internal engineers only for the 'cool' 10% of functionality that provides a unique value to the product.
- Analyze the long-term cost structures of SaaS vendors to ensure the business doesn't lose money as it scales to millions of users.

*Source: [The ultimate guide to Martech | Austin Hay (Reforge, Ramp, Runway)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=B79p85DHLkU) @ 01:08:59*

## Lenny Rachitsky

> "To answer your question about how we balanced this outcome-thinking with engineering and design needs (which often don’t directly drive that outcome), it’s simple — extend your outcome thinking further out into the future. How much would this work benefit your outcome (e.g. growth, quality, retention) 1+ years out? What happens if you don’t do it? What are the chances of it being successful?"

**Insight:** Evaluate major technical or design investments by calculating their long-term ROI on business outcomes and the risks associated with delaying the work.

**Tactical advice:**
- Analyze the impact of technical investments by comparing scenarios of doing the work now versus doing nothing.
- Assess infrastructure projects based on resource consumption, duration, and how they mitigate long-term risks to business strategy.
- Surface technical recommendations to leadership by framing them in terms of short-term goals versus long-term outcome health.

*Source: [This Week #13: Balancing outcome-thinking with design and technical requirements ⚖️](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/this-week-13-balancing-outcome-thinking-with-design-and-technical-requirements)*

## Robby Stein

**Insight:** Knowing when to shift from optimization to new product bets requires identifying the point of diminishing marginal returns on your current S-curve.

**Tactical advice:**
- Analyze the impact of incremental optimizations to determine if you have reached the top of the current product's S-curve.
- Allocate resources toward first-principles bets when existing product improvements no longer drive significant growth.
- Balance the urgency to deliver existing products quickly with the focus needed to explore expansionary new technologies.

*Source: [Inside Google's AI turnaround: The rise of AI Mode, strategy behind AI Overviews, and their vision for AI-powered search | Robby Stein (VP of Product, Google Search)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=kOnsqqVbIeY) @ 00:26:52*

## Ronny Kohavi

**Insight:** Radical redesigns are statistically more likely to fail than incremental iterations, making it safer to decompose big ideas into smaller, testable increments.

**Tactical advice:**
- Prepare teams for an 80% failure rate when launching radical new designs
- Decompose large product changes into smaller increments using OFAT methodology
- Test big bets incrementally to minimize risk and identify exact drivers of impact

*Source: [The ultimate guide to A/B testing | Ronny Kohavi (Airbnb, Microsoft, Amazon)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=hEzpiDuYFoE) @ 00:36:31*

## Tanguy Crusson

**Insight:** Full rewrites of products with active users are extremely risky and often lead to missed market opportunities due to long development delays.

**Tactical advice:**
- Favor incremental improvements over full rewrites for existing user bases.
- Evaluate if a rebuild will cause you to miss a critical competitive window.
- Distinguish between starting a new product and rebuilding an active one.
