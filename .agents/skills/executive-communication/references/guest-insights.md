## Casey Winters

> "One of the ways I try to frame it to my team is if you're not an executive, whatever you're working on, you're basically writing and telling a story. And when you talk to an exec about that story, you have to start with chapter one, which is what part of the company strategy are you working on? What metrics are you trying to improve? What assumptions are you making that are guiding what you're building?"

**Insight:** Effective executive communication requires framing your work as a narrative that explicitly connects project assumptions to high-level company strategy.

**Tactical advice:**
- Start your narrative at the last point in the story that is completely obvious to your audience.
- Explain the company strategy and metrics you are improving before diving into the details of what you built.
- Avoid re-explaining baseline company strategy if the audience is already familiar with it.

*Source: How to sell your ideas and rise within your company | Casey Winters, Eventbrite @ 00:07:11*

## Dylan Field

**Insight:** Executives can be persuaded to adopt features they personally dislike if the team demonstrates that the solution is a fundamental user requirement.

**Tactical advice:**
- Present overwhelming evidence of user demand to override executive concerns about design elegance.
- Acknowledge executive skepticism but emphasize that solving the user's problem takes priority over the 'perfect' implementation.
- Use 'the world' as a proxy for demand when arguing for features that feel utilitarian or inelegant to leadership.

*Source: [Dylan Field live at Config: Intuition, simplicity, and the future of design](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yJQYBjto41s) @ 00:14:49*

## Lenny Rachitsky

> "It’s often more effective to speak their language and demonstrate how my proposal will help them reach their goals, not mine, because stakeholders are focused on their own problems and are more receptive to proposals that address what’s already top of mind for them."

**Insight:** You can overcome executive skepticism by recasting your proposal in a way that prioritizes the leader’s specific philosophy or values.

**Tactical advice:**
- Pivot the framing of your proposal from internal metrics to the leader's primary values.
- Anchor your presentation on insights that mirror the stakeholder's known worldview.
- Speak the stakeholder's language to show how your project solves their specific problems.

*Source: [A PM’s guide to influence](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/a-pms-guide-to-influence)*

> "The trick to sharing bad news effectively is to imagine being on the other side of the table. What would you want to know if you were in charge? If you’re like most people, you’d want to know everything, as soon as possible, plus exactly how to best move forward. So start there."

**Insight:** Building trust when delivering bad news requires proactively providing context, learnings, and a clear path forward rather than just reporting a failure.

**Tactical advice:**
- Ensure stakeholders hear the bad news directly from you first to manage the narrative.

- Highlight specific team learnings and the mistakes that will be avoided in future iterations.

> "While presenting the pros and cons between two priorities, I’d somehow always end up committing to doing *both*, and in less time than I had planned. As you’d expect, this usually went … badly. I’d burn myself out or, worse, burn out my team."

**Insight:** Effective tradeoff communication requires framing choices so that leaders are forced to prioritize one initiative over another instead of defaulting to unrealistic expectations.

**Tactical advice:**
- Frame tradeoffs in terms of company goals rather than team capacity.
- Present clear 'above' and 'below' the line rankings for all projects.
- Stop using defensive language like 'we are thin on resources.'

> "I seek various points in meetings to synthesize the conversation to make various people feel heard and to ensure that everyone is following along and on the same page. I can surface any misunderstandings quickly and bring people back to the crux of the issue so we can make progress. This also helps me secure progressive alignment, which de-risks the decision-making process."

**Insight:** Active synthesis and progressive alignment prevent meetings from getting stuck on misunderstandings and ensure the group reaches a decision within the allotted time.

**Tactical advice:**
- Summarize the conversation periodically to keep the group focused on the main issue.
- Surface and resolve misunderstandings immediately to keep the meeting moving forward.
- Confirm points of agreement incrementally rather than waiting for the end of the meeting.

> "Presence is a combination of verbal cues, body language, and mindset. One of the actions you can take to improve your presence is to stop saying “sorry” when it’s not warranted."

**Insight:** Executive presence is built by replacing submissive language patterns with direct, assertive phrasing that projects mature self-confidence.

**Tactical advice:**
- Replace 'Sorry to bother you' with 'Do you have a minute?' and 'Sorry I messed up' with 'I'll fix that now.'
- Eliminate qualifiers like 'just,' 'actually,' and 'quickly' that unintentionally shrink your power and influence.
- Practice taking control of difficult or unpredictable situations by making timely decisions rather than minimizing your contributions.

*Source: [Getting buy-in](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/getting-buy-in)*

**Insight:** Persuasive storytelling prioritizes the audience's business goals and strips away any context that does not directly support a decision.

**Tactical advice:**
- Structure your message around the specific value the proposal provides to the stakeholder's own metrics.
- Cut the backstory from your presentations to prevent meeting attendees from losing focus or zoning out.
- Limit context to only what is necessary for a decision and move everything else to an appendix.

**Insight:** Securing buy-in for AI investments requires aligning projects with user needs, business viability, and technical feasibility while educating stakeholders on the uncertainties of research.

- Pitch AI initiatives as the intersection of user desirability, business viability, and technical feasibility.

**Tactical advice:**
