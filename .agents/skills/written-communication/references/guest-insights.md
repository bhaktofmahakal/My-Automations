## Julian Shapiro

**Insight:** Injecting original and non-obvious ideas into your writing strengthens the reader's affinity for your mind and encourages them to follow you for your unique perspective.

**Tactical advice:**
- Prioritize writing novel, significant, and non-obvious ideas to capture and hold reader attention.
- Avoid 'fortune cookie' style threads that prioritize clickbait over thoughtful reflection.
- Focus on strengthening the reader's affinity for your unique perspective rather than just gaining volume.

*Source: [Growth tactics, retention strategies, and becoming a better writer | Julian Shapiro (Demand Curve, Hyper, Webflow, TechCrunch)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=9cNRDWEXnrQ) @ 39:03*

Basically if I want to go learn growth or writing well or some other topic, I will go ahead and do a ton of research, read everything I can get my hands on, do a ton of experimentation to try to build a set of novel insights that you couldn't find from other people's research hopefully, and then the next stage is try to make it as concise and actionable as possible so that I can reference it for my own selfish benefit."

## Lenny Rachitsky

**Insight:** A structured approach using specific scripts and templates can bypass the paralysis of fear and make a help request significantly more effective.

**Tactical advice:**
- Structure your ask using a clear template to reduce the emotional friction of making the request.
- Clearly communicate the context and the specific outcome you need from the recipient.
- Practice the skill of asking early and often to build confidence in your professional communication.

*Source: [On asking for help (even when you really don’t want to)](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/on-asking-for-help-even-when-you-really-dont-want-to)*

---

> "If you simplify this further, there are three parts to every narrative: Situation, Complication, Resolution. 1. Situation: What is the state of affairs today. Facts, data, and unambiguous background. 2. Complication: What has changed that requires action. What is the problem? 3. Resolution: What we need to do to resolve this complication."

**Insight:** Structure business narratives around the Situation-Complication-Resolution framework to provide necessary context and urgency before proposing a solution.

**Tactical advice:**
- Describe the current state of affairs using facts and data that everyone can agree on.
- Identify the specific complication or change that necessitates action now.
- Present the resolution as the clear path to solving the identified problem.

*Source: [The Minto Pyramid Principle and the SCR Framework](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/the-minto-pyramid-principle-and-the-scr-framework)*

---

> "Great PMs take pride in the clarity and conciseness of their documents, emails, presentations, and meetings. They know that people judge the quality of their thinking by the quality of their writing and speaking, and that effective communication is the most fundamental PM skill."

**Insight:** Colleagues evaluate the depth and quality of your thinking based on the clarity of your writing and speaking.

**Tactical advice:**
- Ask your manager and trusted peers for brutally honest feedback on your documents and meetings.
- Use frameworks like the Minto Pyramid and SCR when crafting an argument.
- Maintain a 'swipe file' of well-executed emails and presentations to use for future inspiration.

*Source: [14 habits of highly effective product managers](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/14-habits-of-highly-effective-product-managers)*

---

> "The best executive communication starts with the state of affairs. It’s fact-based, unambiguous. It’s totally not controversial. No matter what side of an issue or a hard choice you’re on, you should be able to read the situation and go, ‘That pretty much sums it up.’"

**Insight:** Executive-level clarity is best achieved by establishing a shared, non-controversial 'Situation' before introducing complications or proposals.

**Tactical advice:**
- Start every high-stakes communication with a shared, fact-based 'Situation' that everyone can agree on.
- Follow the Situation with a crisp 'Complication' stating what has changed or is making things harder.
- Present the 'Answer' first at the top of the pyramid, then provide supporting evidence underneath.

*Source: [Essential reading for product builders—part 2](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/essential-reading-for-product-builders-part-2)*

## Zevi Arnovitz

**Insight:** Prevent low-quality 'AI slop' by enforcing a strict planning phase and giving the AI deep context on your style and goals.

**Tactical advice:**
- Onboard the AI like a junior employee by providing context on the writing style and the specific problem you are solving.
- Never let an AI agent start coding without first reviewing and approving a technical plan.
- Give the AI a persona that is instructed to be critical and challenge your 'dumbest ideas' to avoid sycophantic outputs.
