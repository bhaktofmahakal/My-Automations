## Arielle Jackson

> "I personally tend to like those kinds of names that are suggestive or evocative, where when I tell you what the company does, you're like, oh, that makes sense. But it's not that what the name is tells you exactly what the company does."

**Insight:** Effective names should be suggestive and evocative, hinting at the product's value without being overly literal or descriptive.

**Tactical advice:**
- Choose names that are suggestive rather than literal descriptions of the product.
- Focus on names that are short, memorable, and fun to pronounce.
- Seek names that evoke positive emotions or a sense of nostalgia in the audience.

*Source: The art of building legendary brands | Arielle Jackson (Google, Square, Marketer in Residence at First Round Capital) @ 00:12:15*

---

> "So over time, a word can come to mean something that is beyond what that actual word means. Like Disney means magic today. Volvo means safety. Those names are not good."

**Insight:** A brand's strength lies in the specific emotional or functional association it owns in the customer's mind over time.

**Tactical advice:**
- Define the core concept you want your brand to represent, such as 'magic' or 'safety.'
- Understand that a brand name is only one part of an overall strategy that includes product and marketing.
- Focus on delivering consistent value so that your brand name eventually transcends its literal meaning.

*Source: The art of building legendary brands | Arielle Jackson (Google, Square, Marketer in Residence at First Round Capital) @ 00:34:12*

## David Placek

> "Your brand name, nothing's going to be used more often or for longer than that name. Design will change, messaging will change, products will change, but that name is there."

**Insight:** A brand name is the only permanent element of a brand's identity, providing a long-term asymmetric advantage if it is distinctive enough to cut through market noise.

**Tactical advice:**
- Prioritize distinctiveness over descriptiveness to ensure the name stands out in a crowded marketplace.
- Seek a name that starts a story and invites customer engagement rather than one that simply makes a descriptive statement.
- Aim for 'cumulative advantage' by selecting a name that grows stronger as customers build a bond with the brand over time.

*Source: [Naming expert shares the process behind creating billion-dollar brand names like Azure, Vercel, Windsurf, Sonos, Blackberry, and Impossible Burger | David Placek (Lexicon Branding)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=adyIaTopO6g) @ 00:18:35*

---

> "Most clients, they come to a naming project absolutely believing with full confidence that they're going to know it when they see it, and the truth is it almost never happens."

**Insight:** Effective brand selection requires moving beyond the desire for immediate comfort and recognizing that powerful names often create initial internal tension.

**Tactical advice:**
- Reject the assumption that you will 'know the right name when you see it,' as imaginative names often require processing time.
- Use internal team polarization and disagreement as a positive metric for the strength of a potential name.
- Prioritize bold and imaginative names over safe options that offer immediate comfort.

*Source: [Naming expert shares the process behind creating billion-dollar brand names like Azure, Vercel, Windsurf, Sonos, Blackberry, and Impossible Burger | David Placek (Lexicon Branding)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=adyIaTopO6g) @ 00:26:27*

## Jessica Hische

> "One of the things is if, suddenly, you're starting to deal with the greater rollout of the look and feel of the brand. If you, at first, just basically had a really beta website and small version of the app but you're about to do a new one that kind of updates and expands it, that could be a really good time to roll it out."

**Insight:** Time your brand refresh to coincide with major company milestones or physical investments to ensure your visual identity scales with your growth.

**Tactical advice:**
- Audit your current logo for utilitarian issues like poor legibility at small scales or a lack of a clear avatar version.
- Define whether the project scope is a "close-in" technical fix or a "broad" exploration to reach a new audience.
- Transition to custom typography before investing heavily in physical assets like merchandise or conference branding to prevent easy imitation.

*Source: [How to see like a designer: The hidden power of typography and logos | Jessica Hische (Lettering Artist, Author)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tLLqE6Ia8-U) @ 00:06:32*

---

**Insight:** Structure design phases to move from conceptual breadth to technical specificity to ensure the final output aligns with both business vision and functional requirements.

**Tactical advice:**
- Address high-level aesthetic directions and overall look in the first round before moving to "narrower and nerdier" technical details.
- Request or provide design files to be tested "in situ" early in the process to solve utilitarian issues before the final handoff.
- Clearly define project goals—such as whether a refresh should be invisible to customers or signal a major pivot—before starting the creative work.

*Source: [How to see like a designer: The hidden power of typography and logos | Jessica Hische (Lettering Artist, Author)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=tLLqE6Ia8-U) @ 00:50:21*

## Kevin Aluwi

> "I'm a very big believer that the two most important things in a consumer business are product and brand in that order. And I don't think I need to sell the idea, especially to your audience. That product is absolutely critical and probably the most important. But brand as an afterthought is definitely one of the areas where I think there's a giant missed opportunity for consumer tech businesses."

**Insight:** Brand is a critical driver of escape velocity and competitive defensibility for consumer startups and should be prioritized alongside product from the earliest stages.

**Tactical advice:**
- Prioritize brand as a primary pillar of the business alongside product rather than treating it as a later-stage luxury.
- Leverage brand to maintain leadership and differentiation in markets where competitors may have significantly more funding.
- Integrate brand strategy immediately to avoid the missed opportunities of treating it as a squishy afterthought.

## Lenny Rachitsky

> "A great name creates a foundation of trust with consumers, gives meaning and voice to a new idea, and builds cumulative advantage over time. Names that are too descriptive and too comfortable won’t generate the interest or memorability that a new brand needs to succeed."

**Insight:** To build a lasting brand, avoid overly descriptive names in favor of those that spark imagination and establish consumer trust.

**Tactical advice:**
- Set high standards for fresh, energetic ideas rather than narrow constraints like character counts.
- Ensure the name is accessible and easy for the human brain to process.
- Use familiar word components in unexpected ways to inspire both trust and interest.

*Source: [How to find the perfect name](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/how-to-find-the-perfect-name)*

**Tactical advice:**
- Avoid descriptive names that are likely to blend into a crowded competitive landscape.
- Use invented names to signal newness and innovation more effectively than common existing words.
- Choose unexpected name types to capture the attention of audiences who are naturally drawn to the new.

*Source: [How to find the perfect name](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/how-to-find-the-perfect-name)*

> "Quantity breeds quality. Developing a brand name is a treasure hunt. You need to explore, and then explore again. And you need to explore in places where you think there may be no chance of finding the name."

**Insight:** Effective naming requires a high-volume, disciplined exploration process that bypasses the low-energy results of traditional group brainstorming.

**Tactical advice:**
- Generate at least 1,000 creative ideas before narrowing down to a shortlist of 50 to 100 candidates.
- Use the Diamond Framework to define what winning looks like and what the name specifically needs to say.
- Rely on small, high-grit teams rather than large sessions where peer pressure encourages safe, boring choices.

*Source: [How to find the perfect name](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/how-to-find-the-perfect-name)*

> "Imagine the sound of your brand before you start naming. Should your name sound fast? Full? Reliable? ... An invented name with the Latin word for health (“san”) in the middle, a “da” sound that delivers a crisp, clean experience, and an “i” sound that feels light and slim at the end."

**Insight:** Brand names can leverage sound symbolism to subconsciously communicate specific attributes like speed, cleanliness, or reliability to the consumer.

**Tactical advice:**
- Map out the desired 'sound' of your brand (e.g., fast, reliable, or light) before generating word options.
- Use specific letter sounds—like 'z' for a strong signal or 'i' for slimness—to trigger desired psychological responses.
- Experiment with science-based suffixes like '-ium' to communicate that a product is fundamental to performance.

*Source: [How to find the perfect name](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/how-to-find-the-perfect-name)*

## Seth Godin

> "AI very soon is going to stop being a feature the same way electricity is not a feature. What AI companies and all companies need to do is say, what's in this for the user? What promise do I want to make a difficult promise, a remarkable promise, and then how do I keep it?"

**Insight:** A brand is not a logo or a feature set, but a remarkable and difficult promise that a company consistently keeps for its users.

**Tactical advice:**
- Identify the specific utility for the user rather than relying on AI as a novelty feature.
- Define a bold, difficult, and remarkable promise that differentiates your product.
- Build brand equity by consistently delivering on the promise your product makes.

*Source: [Seth Godin's best tactics for building remarkable products, strategies, brands and more](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=UyWTDmDjNy4) @ 00:15:51*
