# Mastering PR and Press - Frameworks, Templates & Checklists

*25 artifacts extracted from Lenny's Podcast and Newsletter*

## Frameworks

### PR as Credibility Asset (Not Lead Gen) (Zoelle Egner)
Use PR coverage strategically as an attachment in hiring emails and cold outbound, not as a direct acquisition channel

How it works: Two recommended goals for PR: 1) Hiring: Include press coverage in candidate outreach emails to increase response rates and signal legitimacy. 2) Cold outbound: Attach coverage to sales emails as credibility proof. Why not lead gen: Most readers won't click the link buried in paragraph 12 of a TechCrunch article. The breadcrumb trail back to conversion is too long. Additional tactic: Instead of one big launch, do a series of smaller launches to maintain novelty, stay top of mind, and enter different communities.

### Seth Godin's 'Remarkable' Test (Generating buzz)
A simple litmus test for whether your launch idea will generate buzz: Is it literally 'worth making a remark about'?

How it works: Core principle: 'Remarkable is a really cool word, because we think it just means neat, but it also means worth making a remark about.' — Seth Godin

The test: Would someone who encounters your product/launch stop what they're doing and tell a friend about it? If not, it's not remarkable enough.

Context from Lenny: 'Your goal is to create something worth remarking about. Something people will stop and pay attention to, and HAVE to share with their friends.'

Supporting insight from Marc Andreessen: 'Everyone's time is already allocated.' With hundreds of products launching each day and endless ads competing for attention, the bar is extremely high.

## Templates

### One-Page Audience Mapping Google Doc (Lulu Cheng Meservey)
A document structure for planning a targeted communications strategy.

How it works: 1. List 5-6 target audiences. 2. Rank them by their ability to influence your success and their credibility. 3. Map their 'cultural erogenous zones' (what they care about). 4. Map where they reside intellectually (podcasts, subreddits, conferences).

## Checklists

### PR Agency Vetting Criteria (Emilie Gerber)
A set of criteria to evaluate potential PR agencies for a startup.

How it works: 1. Don't just look at big logos. 2. Ask what Month 1 looks like (avoid long ramp-ups). 3. Ask for recent writing samples. 4. Meet the actual day-to-day team. 5. Avoid extensive RFPs.

### The 3-Step Press Pitching Process (Jason Feifer)
A high-level workflow for securing media coverage.

How it works: Step 1: Prep (Figure out what press is for and how to tell your story). Step 2: Who to pitch (Find the specific writers/editors receptive to your topic). Step 3: The Pitch (Reaching out with a customized, audience-centric email).

## Examples

### Airbnb's Bottom-Up PR Strategy (Virality is a myth (mostly))
How Airbnb used the 2008 DNC and a deliberate bottom-up press strategy to get broadcast coverage

How it works: Turning point: 2008 Democratic National Convention in Denver (massive housing shortage). Strategy: First pitch only the bloggers with the smallest audience possible. Rationale: Coverage by small press provided social proof that more prominent publications needed to piggyback on the story. Result: Eventually national networks (NBC, CBS) interviewed the founders. The growth was broadcast-driven, not viral person-to-person spread.

### Airbnb's DNC + Obama O's Cereal Press Strategy (How the biggest consumer apps got their first 1,000 users)
How Airbnb leveraged the 2008 Democratic National Convention and custom cereal boxes to generate press and early users

How it works: Phase 1 — Leverage a cultural event:
- 2008 Democratic National Convention in Denver caused massive housing shortage
- Pitched small bloggers first (counterintuitive — not big publications)
- Small blogger coverage provided social proof for larger publications
- Eventually national networks (NBC, CBS) covered the story
- DNC bump was great but only lasted a week

Phase 2 — Create a PR stunt to extend momentum:
- Created fictitious cereals: 'Obama O's, the Cereal of Change' and 'Cap'n McCain's, a Maverick in Every Box'
- Designed box artwork themselves (both RISD graduates)
- Convinced UC Berkeley student to print 500 units of each box cheaply
- Boxes arrived flat — had to be cut and assembled by hand with hot glue
- Sent boxes to hundreds of well-known tech bloggers
- Sold political cereal at $40/box
- Obama O's sold quickly; gave Cap'n McCain's free with each purchase

Chesky quote: 'I was thinking at the time, that Mark Zuckerberg was never hot gluing anything at Facebook — so maybe this is not a good idea.'

### BeReal Growth Timeline: Media Narrative (Growth inflections)
Detailed timeline of how press coverage and the 'anti-Instagram' narrative fueled BeReal's growth

How it works: BeReal growth timeline driven by 'next-generation social media' narrative:
- March 2021: Featured on iOS App Store in France
- April 2021: Featured on Product Hunt (founders asked for post to be taken down)
- May 2021: First significant piece in Financial Times ('Lessons for Big Tech from the anti-social photo app')
- May 2021: TwentyTwo Digital blog post explaining how BeReal works
- June 2021: BeReal subreddit created
- June 2021: Eric Newcomer scoops $30M A16z-led Series A
- June 2021: Vogue writes about social media's new generation
- June 2021: Rex Woodbury writes 'The Startups Reinventing Social Media'

Key context: Pandemic-driven consumer behavior changes + venture dollars in anti-Instagram space (Poparazzi, TTYL, Clubhouse, BeReal) created the narrative opening.

### Butterie Airport Market Research Pitch (Jason Feifer)
An example of pitching a counter-intuitive, problem-solving story rather than a product feature.

How it works: Joelle Mertzel pitched Entrepreneur Magazine not on her hinged butter dish product, but on how she saved $10,000 on market research by surveying bored people waiting at airport gates. This fit the publication's mission of teaching entrepreneurs how to think through challenges.

### Canva — Leveraging High-Profile Investors for Press (How today's fastest growing B2B businesses found their first ten customers)
How Canva used investor credibility to generate press coverage and build a 50K waitlist before launch

How it works: Had high-profile investors that generated press around their 'stealth startup'. Built up a 50K person waitlist before launch. Focused on ensuring those waitlisted users would like the product when they launched.

### Instagram's Coordinated Press Launch (Virality is a myth (mostly))
How Instagram lined up press coverage for simultaneous launch-day broadcast

How it works: People told the founders there was no point contacting the New York Times. They ignored this advice. Not only did the NYT talk to them, they sent someone to meet with them. On launch day (October 2010), all the press coverage happened at roughly the same time, hitting their servers hard. This was a coordinated one-to-many broadcast event, not organic viral spread.

### Meg O'Hara's Cold Email Pitch (Jason Feifer)
A successful cold email pitch that resulted in a podcast episode and article.

How it works: Subject: 'Idea for entrepreneur and Problem Solvers: how the Border Closures grew my business.' Structure: 1. Acknowledged the specific show/magazine. 2. Hooked with a counter-intuitive premise (benefiting from COVID border closures). 3. Used bullet points for 'Problem' and 'Solution', matching the exact format of the target podcast.

Examples of companies that orchestrated PR stunts for attention and growth

### Ripple Rug Trend Pitch (Jason Feifer)
An example of getting press by pitching a broader industry trend or problem rather than your specific product.

How it works: Fred Ruckel initially pitched his cat toy (rejected). He followed up by pitching a story about Amazon-to-eBay arbitrage scams that were hurting small businesses. He became the main character of a 4,000-word feature, getting press for his company indirectly by providing context to a larger issue.

### Salesforce 'End of Software' Guerrilla Launch (Marc Benioff)
A legendary marketing stunt used to launch Salesforce and hijack a competitor's event.

How it works: Hired actors as protesters with 'End of software' signs outside Siebel's user conference. When Siebel called the cops, Salesforce deployed fake news crews (KNMS - K No More Software) to interview the protesters, creating a massive media moment, followed by a huge launch party.

### Slack's Press Blitz Launch (How to find and win your first 10 B2B customers)
Slack used the founding team's prior experience and reputation to execute an impressive press campaign that drove 8,000 day-one invitation requests

How it works: Approach:
1. Leveraged the team's prior experience and reputation (from Flickr) to generate press interest
2. Executed an impressive press blitz
3. Used an invitation-request model (people requested an invitation to try Slack)

Results:
- Day 1: 8,000 invitation requests
- 2 weeks later: 15,000 invitation requests

Lesson: 'Don't underestimate the power of traditional media when you launch' — but note this is rare and was based on an already-famous founding team.

### Superhuman's Cultural Zeitgeist Press Strategy (How the biggest consumer apps got their first 1,000 users)
How Superhuman generated 5,000+ signups from a single strategically timed Medium post about the Mailbox shutdown

How it works: Strategy: Pick 1-2 events per year where you can insert yourself into the cultural zeitgeist.

Execution:
- When Mailbox (competitor email app) was shut down, Rahul wrote an article on how to survive an acquisition
- Published on Medium, syndicated to qz.com
- Became one of the most widely read articles on the topic
- Total effort: ~3 days writing + 1 day shopping it around = 4 days total
- Result: North of 5,000 signups

Article: 'RIP Mailbox, or: Founders, How to Stop Worrying and Love Being Acquired'

### The Mom Project's Press-Driven Growth (Kickstarting supply in a labor marketplace)
How pitching an individual user story to local press created viral supply acquisition

How it works: At launch, The Mom Project pitched the story of Phoebe, a mom on their marketplace who had left her job at Google, to the Chicago Tribune. Phoebe's story resonated so strongly with women that the article became syndicated across other outlets and mom blogs. Result: thousands of users acquired overnight from a single press story. Key tactic: pitch individual user stories, not just the company story.

### Twilio — Creative PR Stunt for TechCrunch Launch (How today's fastest growing B2B businesses found their first ten customers)
How Twilio used a rickroll stunt to get its first press hit before its TechCrunch launch

How it works: In November 2008, Twilio launched on TechCrunch in private beta. A couple days before, angel investor Dave McClure (founder of 500 Startups) used the Twilio service to rickroll Michael Arrington of TechCrunch, resulting in the company's first press hit. This creative stunt demonstrated the product's capabilities while generating attention.

### Venmo 'Lucas' Subway Campaign (Generating buzz)
Venmo's mysterious subway poster campaign featuring 'Lucas uses Venmo' that led to a 40% increase in daily signups

How it works: Venmo plastered mysterious 'Lucas uses Venmo' posters throughout the NYC subway system. The cryptic, unexplained nature of the posters generated curiosity and conversation. Result: 40% increase in daily signups. This is an example of the 'Be remarkably everywhere' strategy — saturating a specific physical location with a memorable, curiosity-provoking message.
