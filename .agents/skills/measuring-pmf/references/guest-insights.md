## Adam Grenier

> "Start by assuming you no longer have product market fit, because you had product market fit in a different market. It's a different market now, so you have to start over."

**Insight:** Macroeconomic shifts can fundamentally alter your customer base, making previous product-market fit obsolete and requiring a ground-up re-evaluation.

**Tactical advice:**
- Assume current product-market fit is lost when entering a significantly changed market environment.
- Identify how the entire customer base has changed rather than assuming a new marketing channel will fix growth issues.
- Prepare to adjust core product features and positioning rather than just scaling existing tactics.

*Source: [When and how to invest in new acquisition channels | Adam Grenier (Uber, MasterClass)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-PDsvl2WCZU) @ 00:38:26*

## Aparna Chennapragada

**Insight:** For early-stage products, prioritize immediate, qualitative micro-feedback from live usage over macro metrics to find core resonance.

**Tactical advice:**
- Focus on finding 'punchline market fit' by optimizing for the moment the core value proposition clicks for the user.
- Actively seek out 'tough feedback' on early versions to build product resilience and iterate faster.
- Use small-scale experiments to gather micro-feedback before moving to a general availability rollout.

## Jag Duggal

> "We rarely scale a project, a product we've launched, until we know the Sean Ellis score and we know that it's hit a threshold that we find really compelling."

**Insight:** Use a standardized quantitative threshold, such as the Sean Ellis score, to objectively validate product-market fit before investing in significant expansion.

**Tactical advice:**
- Ask users how disappointed they would be on a three-point scale if the product went away.
- Set a strict threshold—such as 40% or 50% 'very disappointed'—as a requirement for scaling.
- Adjust scoring benchmarks based on regional cultural biases in survey responses.
