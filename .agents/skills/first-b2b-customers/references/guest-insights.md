## Ben Williams

> "They started with a really narrow, early focus. It was a single persona, single context, single use case, and what that meant for Snyk was developers building applications using Node.js who wanted to ensure that the open source dependencies they were pulling into their apps were secure."

**Insight:** Early traction for a developer tool is best achieved by solving a hyper-specific problem for a single programming language community rather than launching a broad, horizontal solution.

**Tactical advice:**
- Identify a single persona and a single use case to solve deeply before expanding to a wider market.
- Engage directly with the community by presenting at dedicated niche conferences and meetups.
- Frame the product's value as a solution to a provocative question that highlights a hidden vulnerability or problem in the user's workflow.

*Source: [How Snyk built a product-led growth juggernaut | Ben Williams (VP of Product at Snyk)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=21sFTZzIfUk) @ 00:09:47*

## Jen Abel

**Insight:** Identifying the right initial sales leads requires narrowing the market to those currently feeling the specific problem your novel insight addresses.

**Tactical advice:**
- Target prospects who are most likely to experience the specific pain point your subject matter expertise solves.
- Focus your initial search on finding leads where you can demonstrate immediate relevancy to their specific job title or role.

*Source: ["Sell the alpha, not the feature": The enterprise sales playbook for $1M to $10M ARR | Jen Abel](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=37fKFWdrMyA) @ 00:23:28*

---

**Insight:** When selling to non-consumers or immature buyers, founders may need to provide strategic guidance or scoping services before the prospect is ready to purchase software.

**Tactical advice:**
- Evaluate if the prospect has a mature enough buying process to go straight to a software demo.
- Consider using the proposal stage to collaboratively define the prospect's strategy if they are not yet a regular consumer of your category.

*Source: ["Sell the alpha, not the feature": The enterprise sales playbook for $1M to $10M ARR | Jen Abel](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=37fKFWdrMyA) @ 00:43:16*

## Karri Saarinen

**Insight:** Developing features alongside a few key customers allows for real-world validation and iteration before a general release.

**Tactical advice:**
- Partner with specific, high-growth companies to co-create and refine new features based on actual needs.
- Be transparent with early testers that features are unfinished to encourage raw feedback.
- Use opt-in beta programs to create a feedback loop with your most engaged users.

## Lenny Rachitsky

**Insight:** Achieving early PMF depends on viewing initial customers' trust as a sacred obligation and providing extreme, high-touch support to ensure their success.

**Tactical advice:**
- Establish a 'red alert' internal response for any bug affecting your first users.
- Obsess over making your first alpha customer successful at all costs.
- Monitor usage charts daily to catch and fix adoption issues before the customer complains.

> "It turns out there are just three strategies early-on: 1. Bottom-up + self-service: Users discover your product, sign up for a free plan, love it, and upgrade themselves in order to get more functionality. 2. Bottom-up + inside sales: Users discover your product, sign up for a free plan, and then are either encouraged by a sales rep (or pro-actively reach out) to upgrade for more functionality. 3. Outbound sales: Founders or early sales hires reach out to leads, help them trial the product, and then sign them up for a paid plan."

**Insight:** B2B companies typically convert early users through one of three primary motions: bottom-up self-service, bottom-up with inside sales support, or outbound founder-led sales.

**Tactical advice:**
- Select a bottom-up motion if your product is simple enough for users to discover and upgrade independently.
- Use inside sales to proactively reach out to free users who show high engagement but need a nudge to upgrade.
- Start with outbound sales if your product requires a trial period and high-touch guidance from the founders.

*Source: [How today's fastest-growing B2B startups turned their early users into paying customers](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/how-todays-fastest-growing-b2b-startups-turned-their-early-users-into-paying-cus)*

## Sri Batchu

> "My view is you start off with founder-led sales, the early team needs to know how to actually sell. Then you hire your first couple of salespeople, then you start some very low cost targeted marketing efforts. So whether it's content, community, small scale events, and then PR, after all of that is when you start paid and brand effort and then SEO probably start around the same time that you start paid marketing efforts. The reason for the progression the way I've described it is the channels get more expensive as you go farther along and they get more effective as you understand more about your customers."

**Insight:** Sequence B2B growth by starting with manual, founder-led sales to build customer knowledge before scaling into progressively more expensive and automated marketing channels.

**Tactical advice:**
- Master founder-led sales before hiring your first dedicated salespeople.
- Launch low-cost, targeted marketing like content and community events before scaling paid channels.
- Recruit influential operators and early adopters as investors to use your cap table as a growth strategy.

*Source: [Lessons from scaling Ramp | Sri Batchu (Ramp, Instacart, Opendoor)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=RcYCU5UAZOk) @ 00:06:16*

## Todd Jackson

**Insight:** The earliest stage of product-market fit requires securing deep customer satisfaction from a small cohort even if the delivery is entirely manual.

**Tactical advice:**
- Focus exclusively on achieving deep customer satisfaction for a handful of initial users.
- Prioritize solving an important and urgent problem over building for operational efficiency.
- Ensure the core solution is proven and satisfying before attempting to build for broad demand.

*Source: [A framework for finding product-market fit | Todd Jackson (First Round Capital)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yc1Uwhfxacs) @ 00:21:25*
