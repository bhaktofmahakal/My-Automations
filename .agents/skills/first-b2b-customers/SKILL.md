# Acquiring First B2B Customers

Transition from zero to your first ten paying customers through manual hustle and high-trust relationships.

Help the user with acquiring first b2b customers using insights from 7 guests and posts across Lenny's Podcast and Newsletter.

## How to Help

1. **Assess network and trust** - Evaluate the user's existing connections to identify the highest-probability early adopters.
2. **Refine the initial pitch** - Help craft messages for cold outreach or network requests that emphasize advice-seeking and problem-solving.
3. **Design the partner program** - Guide the creation of a design partner framework to co-develop features with early users.
4. **Plan the manual rollout** - Strategize high-touch onboarding and support sequences to ensure initial customer success.

## Core Principles

### Hyper-specific niche focus
Ben Williams: "They started with a really narrow, early focus. It was a single persona, single context, single use case, and what that meant for Snyk was developers building applications using Node.js who wanted to ensure that the open source dependencies they were pulling into their apps were secure."

Achieve early traction by solving a single problem for a narrow persona before expanding to the wider market.

### Founder-led sales mastery
Sri Batchu: "My view is you start off with founder-led sales, the early team needs to know how to actually sell. Then you hire your first couple of salespeople, then you start some very low cost targeted marketing efforts. So whether it's content, community, small scale events, and then PR, after all of that is when you start paid and brand effort and then SEO probably start around the same time that you start paid marketing efforts. The reason for the progression the way I've described it is the channels get more expensive as you go farther along and they get more effective as you understand more about your customers."

Master the sales process manually as a founder to build deep customer knowledge before attempting to scale or hire a sales team.

### The Network Advantage
From "How today's fastest growing B2B businesses found their first ten customers": "In practice, it’s even easier — almost every B2B business BOTH hits up their personal network AND heads to the places their potential customers were spending time. The question isn’t which of these two routes to pursue, but instead how far your own network will take you before you move on."

Leverage personal networks first because they provide the baseline of trust necessary for a buyer to take a risk on an early-stage product.

### Targeted creative outreach
From "How to find and win your first 10 B2B customers": "One of the recurring themes (and biggest surprises) across my interviews is the value, and effectiveness, of going cold outbound in the early stages—cold emails, cold DMs, cold calling. But you need to be clever about how you do this."

Use highly personalized and creative cold outreach targeted at specific strategic individuals rather than using automated or generic scripts.

## Questions to Help Users

- "Who are the top ten people in your personal network who feel the specific pain you are solving?"
- "What is the single most urgent problem your product solves for a specific persona?"
- "Are you willing to perform manual work behind the scenes to make the product feel magical for your first customer?"
- "Which online or offline communities do your potential buyers congregate in most frequently?"
- "How can you frame your initial outreach as a request for advice rather than a sales pitch?"

## Common Mistakes to Flag

- **Scaling sales too early** - Hiring sales reps before the founder has personally mastered the sales process leads to a lack of customer insight and failed quotas.
- **Broad targeting** - Attempting to sell a horizontal solution to everyone at once dilutes the product's value and makes it harder to build a concentrated base of happy users.
- **Automated cold outreach** - Sending generic, bulk emails to cold leads destroys trust and results in low conversion compared to high-effort, personalized messages.
- **Ignoring the power of manual support** - Failing to provide extreme, high-touch support in the early days prevents the deep learning required to achieve true product-market fit.
