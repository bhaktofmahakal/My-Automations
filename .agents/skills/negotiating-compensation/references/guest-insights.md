## Lenny Rachitsky

**Insight:** Accurate benchmarking requires comparing your base salary to specific percentiles within your functional level rather than just looking at the broad average.

**Tactical advice:**
- Compare your current base salary to the 25th, 50th, and 75th percentiles for your specific job level.
- Analyze benchmarks based on your functional role and geography, as these have the highest impact on salary.
- Use the median rather than the average to avoid being misled by extreme salary outliers.

*Source: [How much do U.S. product managers really make?](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/how-much-do-us-product-managers-really-make)*

> "Every recruiter worth their salt will ask about your salary expectations when you first start interviewing. Do not — I repeat, do not — give them a number. What to do instead: Ask for the range they’re budgeted for the role."

**Insight:** Salary negotiation is a multi-stage process that begins during initial interviews and relies on gathering specific intelligence to build a collaborative case for higher compensation.

**Tactical advice:**
- Ask for the budgeted salary band instead of providing your own expectations.
- Mine for intel during interviews by asking about team priorities and role challenges to use as ammunition later.
- Time your interviews so you receive multiple offers around the same time to increase your leverage.

*Source: [The 10 commandments of salary negotiation](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/the-10-commandments-of-salary-negotiation)*

> "When evaluating a job offer, the most common priority for tech and other professionals is clear: salary. A significant 75% of respondents selected salary over equity as their top priority during negotiations (n=5,035)."

**Insight:** Modern tech workers have shifted toward prioritizing salary over equity as they seek financial stability amid inflation and economic uncertainty.

**Tactical advice:**
- Prioritize base salary to ensure daily expenses and high costs of living are covered.
- Evaluate non-financial levers such as work-life flexibility and learning funds when salary or equity are capped.
- View equity as a long-term 'lottery ticket' rather than a guaranteed source of income for paying bills.

*Source: [Why cash is king](https://www.lennysnewsletter.com/p/why-cash-is-king)*

> "At Tesla, Microsoft, Nvidia, Apple, Meta, Google, and Netflix (the Magnificent 7), the starting base salary for a (non-APM) PM is $136,000, $156,000, $158,000, $158,000, $167,000, $199,000, and $343,000, respectively. Netflix and Tesla are low on equity, while Meta, Google, and Nvidia are quite high."

**Insight:** Even among the highest-paying tech giants, compensation philosophies vary wildly between high-cash models and high-equity models.

**Tactical advice:**
- Use Netflix's $343k starting base as a benchmark if you are negotiating for a cash-heavy offer.
- Target Meta, Google, or Nvidia if your goal is to maximize total comp through aggressive equity grants.
- Clarify whether an entry-level offer is for an APM or a standard PM role, as this significantly impacts the starting base.

> "The biggest salary jump happens going from L4 to L5 and M3 to M4. And then from VP to CPO."

**Insight:** Understanding the 'jump' levels in standard leveling frameworks helps identify when you have the most leverage for a significant compensation increase.

**Tactical advice:**
- Map your current title to the P1-P6 (IC) and M3-M6 (Manager) framework to see where you sit in market benchmarks.
- Prioritize reaching L5 (Senior) or M4 (Manager) to unlock the most significant mid-career compensation increases.
- Use standardized levels rather than company-specific titles when comparing offers to ensure an apples-to-apples evaluation.

> "But in reality, the more important relationship is between level and salary, and years of experience is only a correlated variable to job level. ... Levels are difficult to standardize across different companies and industries. Even among the big tech companies, levels don’t necessarily map one-to-one."

**Insight:** Effective compensation benchmarking requires standardizing job levels into a consistent hierarchy because years of experience is a poor predictor of actual market value.

**Tactical advice:**
- Map internal company titles to a standardized hierarchy of entry-level, mid-level, and senior-level categories.
- Prioritize 'job level' over 'years of experience' when evaluating or setting salary benchmarks.
- Use functional job titles as a proxy to compare compensation across different company sizes and industries.

> "Ask the questions an investor would ask because, *news flash*, you are now an investor — but instead of cash, you’re staking your time and earning trajectory on the company’s success. You can meet with the investors too; it’s 100% OK to ask for that when the company is early-stage."

**Insight:** Evaluating startup equity requires an investor's mindset, focusing on underlying data like valuation, strike price, and financing rather than just the number of shares.

**Tactical advice:**
- Request specific data points including the option strike price, preferred price, and total number of outstanding shares.
- Inquire about the company's current state of financing, runway, and path to profitability.
- Ask for meetings with the company's investors if you are considering joining at an early stage.

> "Initial offer comes in low: The team may have felt that you have a lot of “room for growth.” In this case, my advice is to dig deeper and ask the interviewer to share feedback from folks who met you to fix any misconceptions before you ever negotiate."

**Insight:** Understanding where an initial offer sits within a company's salary band allows you to strategically address performance feedback or push for a higher level.

**Tactical advice:**
- Ask for specific feedback from the interview loop if the offer is lower than expected to correct misconceptions before negotiating.
- Wait to negotiate middle-of-the-road offers until you have matched with a specific team and have a manager advocate.
- Push for an 'out-of-band' offer if your initial offer already sits at the top of the current level's band.
