# Running Effective Meetings - Frameworks, Templates & Checklists

*47 artifacts extracted from Lenny's Podcast and Newsletter*

## Frameworks

### Bezos's Meeting Principles (Tamar Yehoshua)
Jeff Bezos's approach to executive meetings: six-pagers instead of PowerPoint, leader speaks last after hearing from everyone, and consistency of principles so the organization can predict what matters.

How it works: 1) Write six-pagers (like studying for a final exam). 2) In meetings, go around the table — leader speaks last. 3) Maintain consistent principles (customer-driven, no icons without labels, long-term thinking). 4) Remember details between meetings. 5) Reframe constraints as advantages ('That is your advantage' when told competitors had 10x resources). Principle: Consistency of principles enables large organizations to operate more effectively.

### Brainwriting (Jonathan Lowenhar)
An asynchronous alternative to brainstorming designed to eliminate the 'founder effect' and level the playing field for introverts.

How it works: Expose an idea or problem, have everyone write and weigh in asynchronously (ideally without authorship identified), read the document silently at the start of the meeting, and then debate.

### Cross-Functional Alignment Meeting Structure (Ray Cao)
TikTok's quarterly 180-person cross-functional meetings using Amazon-style document reading format to align engineering, product, sales, and data science teams

How it works: Structure: (1) Quarterly cadence (was monthly). (2) ~180 people including engineering, product, sales, and data science — not just leaders, down to IC level. (3) Doc-reading format: Everyone reads the same document, adds comments. (4) Discussion focuses on: decisions needed, blockers identified, and celebrations. (5) Supplemented by immersion trips where PMs and engineers join sellers to meet clients face-to-face. Purpose: Ensure cross-functional context sharing so engineers know market needs, PMs know client feedback, and sellers understand product roadmap.

### Daily Meeting Execution Model (Mayur Kamat)
A process for solving urgent, massive problems by assigning a single owner and holding daily leadership syncs to ensure rapid unblocking.

How it works: Steps: 1. Identify an urgent, existential problem. 2. Assign a single owner at a nightly leadership call. 3. Owner dedicates all hands on deck to the problem. 4. Hold daily syncs (including weekends/holidays) to report updates. 5. Ensure no decision is blocked for more than 24 hours.

### Data Office Hours (Jess Lachs)
A dedicated weekly time block for the data team to support the rest of the company.

How it works: Two hours every week where non-technical users can drop in to get help writing SQL, reviewing work, or brainstorming with data partners.

### Discover, Discuss, Decide (Annie Duke)
A model for structuring group decisions where discovery and deciding are done asynchronously, and meetings are reserved strictly for discussion.

How it works: 1. Discover: Elicit opinions independently (e.g., forced rank via forms). 2. Discuss: Meet to explore areas of disagreement. 3. Decide: Vote or have the DRI make the call independently.

### Five-Star Meetings (Joe Hudson)
A diagnostic tool for leaders to uncover organizational dysfunction by optimizing meeting enjoyment.

How it works: Commit to making every meeting a 'five-star' experience where people walk out feeling energized. The specific meetings that cannot be made enjoyable will point directly to the root problems, misalignments, or personnel issues in the company.

### Leadership Meeting Structure (Tactical vs. Strategic) (Ideal sprint length, designer vs. PM roles, running PM team meetings, running post-mortems, best product/executive coaches, and much more)
A framework for splitting leadership meetings into tactical and strategic cadences to avoid status-report syndrome

How it works: Two types of leadership meetings:

1. TACTICAL (Weekly):
   - Quick decisions on key events already decided
   - Spotting icebergs / risks
   - Progress reports are REMOVED from live meetings — sent async via Slack/docs with comment capability
   - One person runs the meeting and decides on 1-2 topics to discuss and unblock

2. STRATEGIC (Two sub-types):
   a. Monthly Strategic:
      - Focus on pillars that are 1-2 quarter focused
      - Pricing/packaging decisions affecting GTM motions
      - Top 1-3 decision items only (more than 3? Schedule another meeting)
   b. Annual/Yearly Strategic:
      - What does success look like for next year?
      - Priorities, headcount management, budget planning

Key principles:
- #1 goal is ALIGNMENT — everyone in lockstep
- Everyone gets to bring a topic for decision
- Every function should be represented
- Communicate filtered minutes to org (not raw notes — employees lack full context)
- Time with leaders in a room is precious — don't waste it on readouts

Recommended system: Entrepreneurial Operating System (EOS) from 'Traction' by Gino Wickman — keeps tasks aligned, people accountable, aligned to quarterly/annual 'rocks'.

### Meetingageddon (Farhan Thawar)
An annual process to delete all internal recurring meetings with more than two people to reset calendar inertia.

How it works: Delete all recurring meetings >2 people (excluding 1-on-1s and external meetings). Implement a two-week moratorium on creating new recurring meetings to force teams to evaluate if the meeting is truly needed.

### Note-and-Vote Decision-Making Technique (Introducing the Foundation Sprint: From the creators of the Design Sprint)
A fast group decision-making method that prevents groupthink by requiring individual silent thinking before group discussion

How it works: Steps:
1. WRITE: Give everyone 5 minutes to think in silence and write answers on separate sticky notes (one idea per note)
2. REVIEW: Review all answers in silence — no discussion yet
3. VOTE: Use dot stickers to vote for the best options (silent voting)
4. DEBATE: The Decider calls for a short debate
5. DECIDE: The Decider makes a final decision (regardless of the votes)

Key principle: Individual thinking before group discussion prevents anchoring bias and ensures diverse ideas surface. The Decider has final authority regardless of vote tallies.

Used for: choosing target customer, choosing customer problem, identifying advantage, listing competitors, writing differentiators, writing principles, generating approaches, and throughout the Foundation Sprint.

### One-Hour Slack Sprint (Laura Modi)
An asynchronous decision-making process used to replace traditional meetings.

How it works: Dedicate exactly one hour over Slack for a group (e.g., 20 people) to go back and forth on a specific topic, with the strict requirement that a final decision is made by the end of the hour.

### PM Team Meeting Frequency Guide (Leading a PM team meeting - Issue 19)
Guidelines for choosing the right cadence for PM team meetings based on team size

How it works: Recommended frequency: 1-2 times per month.
- The bigger the team, the less frequent the meeting should be.
- Weekly PM team meetings become redundant with your 1:1s.
- Meeting less than monthly can be valuable for social/bonding purposes, but most other benefits (alignment, understanding) go away.
- Rule: No laptops allowed.

### Silent Read Meetings (Kevin Yien)
A meeting format to gather document feedback where attendees spend the first 20 minutes silently reading and commenting on a document together in real-time.

How it works: Schedule a Zoom/room, cameras can be off, everyone reads the doc, author replies to comments in real-time, leaving 2 minutes at the end for verbal discussion.

### Summarization as Strategy (Anneka Gupta)
A meeting facilitation tactic to drive clarity, build consensus, and be perceived as strategic.

How it works: Steps: 1. Pause the discussion. 2. State: 'Let me pause here and try to capture what has been said.' 3. Summarize the customer challenges, proposed solutions, and right to win. 4. End with a question: 'Is everyone in agreement with that or is there dissent?' Can be done verbally, on a whiteboard, or in Zoom chat.

### Three Goals of a PM Team Meeting (Leading a PM team meeting - Issue 19)
A framework for defining the purpose of a PM team meeting, with three canonical goals to choose from and make explicit

How it works: The three typical goals for a PM team meeting:
1. Understanding: Make sure everyone feels like they know what's going on, inside and outside the team.
2. Alignment: Make sure everyone on the team is aligned.
3. Connection: Tighten the bonds between everyone on your team.

If there are other goals, great. If it's just a single goal, even better. Make these goals explicit when creating the meeting, and continue to check in on whether you're achieving them.

### WWW (Who/What/When) Action Clarity Test (Introducing the GAIN framework for feedback: an evidence-based approach to giving feedback that people love, appreciate, and act on)
A simple three-word test to ensure action items from any meeting or conversation are actually actionable

How it works: For every action item, clarify: WHO will do WHAT by WHEN?

Watch for red flag words:
- 'Let's' → Who specifically? Is that you or me?
- 'Should' → Is that an idea we're considering or a commitment?

When you hear these words, use them as an automatic signal to pause and clarify who will do what by when.

Jack calls this 'the question that moves growth, progress, and whole companies forward faster than any other one I know.'

### YC Office Hours Question Framework (Gustaf Alstromer, Summary: Lessons from working with 600+ YC startups | Gustaf Alströmer (Y Combinator, Airbnb))
A specific set of questions used to run highly effective individual and group accountability meetings for founders.

How it works: Regular Office Hours (1-on-1):
- Key question: 'What's holding you back from moving faster?'

Group Office Hours (multiple startups together):
- Key question 1: 'What are your goals for the next two weeks?'
- Key question 2: 'What were your goals for the last two weeks?'
- Key question 3: 'Did you hit the goals?'
- Key question 4: 'If not, what came in the way of hitting the goals?'

Benefits: Addresses founder loneliness, creates biweekly accountability cadence, surfaces blockers.

Setup tip: Founders in coworking spaces must deliberately schedule time for interaction—by default people work in isolation with headphones on. Best conducted in-person with full attention and no distractions.

## Templates

### Dory/Pulse Meeting Ritual (Shishir Mehrotra)
A two-part meeting ritual that eliminates groupthink: Pulse collects hidden written opinions simultaneously, and Dory prioritizes questions through voting before discussion.

How it works: Pulse: In write-ups and meetings, instead of going around the room, everyone writes down their opinion simultaneously with others' responses hidden until complete. Forces eloquent, on-the-record, unbiased input. Dory (named after the fish who asks all questions): Questions are submitted in writing, then the group upvotes/downvotes to determine discussion priority. Enhanced version (borrowed from Coinbase's Rapids): Add a 'Role' column to the Pulse table (approver, informed, decider, etc.), group by role, and the meeting runner pre-fills what they need from each person.

### Exquisite Exec Team Meeting Format (Matt Mochary)
A meeting structure designed to reduce a 3-hour meeting to 45 minutes by using asynchronous pre-reads.

How it works: Requires pre-prepared written updates from all attendees including: progress against priorities (red/yellow/green), last week's actions, this week's actions, identified problems, and proposed solutions. The first 15 minutes of the meeting are spent reading silently.

### Linktree Meeting Replacement Rituals (How to make an impact in your first 90 days)
A set of meeting rituals used to replace 50% of meetings while improving shipping velocity, transparency, and founder alignment

How it works: Step 1: Audit meetings and identify which ones people hate. Axe 50% to create more maker time.
Step 2: Consolidate around a few key rituals:
- Demo Power Hour: Solves for higher shipping velocity. Helps everyone feel ownership and pride in their work.
- Scorecard: Solves for transparency and accountability.
- Founder Jam Sessions: Solves for alignment and working more closely with founders. Includes PMs, designers, and engineering leads.

### Meeting Alignment Script (Chip Conley)
A script to open meetings with demanding or easily sidetracked leaders to ensure the conversation stays on track.

How it works: Start the meeting by saying: 'Let's talk about what we're trying to accomplish here. Let's get really clear on what's the intention of this iteration... What defines success, and what do I want to get accomplished in this meeting?' Use this to anchor the leader when they go off-topic.

### Meeting Operating System Versioning (Nikhyl Singhal)
A process for continuously improving how a team meets and makes decisions.

How it works: Treat meetings like a product. Every 90 days, release a new 'version' that defines: the meetings, the discussions, how the team organizes, attendees, decision-making frameworks, weekly cadence, and hybrid/WFH rules. Take feedback after 2 months and iterate.

### Monthly PM Cross-Functional Meeting Agenda (Ideal sprint length, designer vs. PM roles, running PM team meetings, running post-mortems, best product/executive coaches, and much more)
An alternative monthly meeting format for PM teams focused on KPIs, experiments, and deep dives

How it works: Monthly PM Meeting Agenda (for business-as-usual periods):

1. Review of top-level company goals — How are we doing against them?
2. PM Round Robin (5 min each) — Each PM shares:
   - Their key KPI(s)
   - Experiments run
   - Lessons learned
   - Upcoming launches
3. Guest Speaker Deep Dive — One PM presents either:
   - A challenge they are facing, OR
   - A key decision they need to make
   - Group provides feedback and deep dives

Note: Separate weekly product review meetings (each PM had one) with product leadership were approval-focused. Any PM could attend most of them and usually did to listen in.

### PM Team Meeting Agenda Template (Ideal sprint length, designer vs. PM roles, running PM team meetings, running post-mortems, best product/executive coaches, and much more, Leading a PM team meeting - Issue 19)
A structured weekly PM team meeting agenda designed to increase transparency, psychological safety, and PM output

How it works: Weekly PM Team Meeting Agenda:

1. General Announcements — Company direction, new company-level decisions
2. Follow-ups from Last Meeting — Track action items
3. Recent / Upcoming Launches — Celebrate launches + raise awareness for implications to other parts of product
4. New Efforts / Updates to Existing Efforts — New efforts or direction changes for visibility to other PMs (only ones beneficial for other PMs, not every project)
5. 'What Feels Hard?' / BB Time (5-10 min) — Vulnerable discussion of sticking points: troubleshooting comms issues, tearing down stuck PRDs, trying on versions of a vision/strategy. Chatham House Rules apply. Spin off into separate threads if discussion balloons.
6. Product Brief Peer Review — One PM presents a product brief for immediate feedback from the group. Elevates writing clarity and streamlines team communications.
7. Helpful Reads / Info — Slack threads, articles, resources for PMs
8. Open Floor — Anyone can speak; leader models open listening and responding without judgment
9. Todos — Capture action items

Key principles:
- Model vulnerability and open listening from the top
- Maintain Chatham House Rules for 'what's hard' discussion
- Budget ~5-10 min for the vulnerability segment
- Start informal when team is small; make it intentional as team grows

### Recurring Remote Meeting Document Structure (This Week #6: Cultivating good relationships with distributed co-workers, building trust to accelerate growth, and leveling up as a PM when you have extra time)
A single-document format for recurring meetings that combines agenda and structured notes

How it works: For each recurring meeting, maintain a single document with the following structure per meeting instance:

- Date: [date]
- Attendees: [names]
- Agenda: [topics to discuss]
- Meeting Notes:
  - Options discussed: [list of options considered]
  - Decisions made: [list of decisions]
  - Next steps: [list of action items]
  - Owners: [who is responsible for each action item]

This document grows over time, providing a running record of all discussions, decisions, and commitments.

### Strategy Salons / Nerd Clubs (Alex Komoroske)
A format for running secret, cross-functional brainstorming communities.

How it works: 1. Invite only people who intrinsically want to be there. 2. Enforce a strict 'yes, and' rule (ignore bad ideas, build on good ones using 'I wonder...'). 3. Dribble in diverse new perspectives slowly. 4. Maintain momentum by ensuring quorum and sending FOMO-inducing notes to absentees.

### Tag-up Meeting (Lane Shackleton)
A group 1:1 meeting structure for the core product triad to discuss project work and unblock decisions.

How it works: Attendees include PM, Eng Lead, Design Lead, and sometimes Sales/Marketing. Starts with OKR/metrics review, followed by a 'Dory' (an upvoted table of topics added by anyone) to drive the discussion.

## Checklists

### 4-Step AI-Augmented Meeting Synthesis Process (How to become a supermanager with AI)
A structured process for using AI to improve meeting notes while keeping PMs actively engaged in synthesis rather than outsourcing thinking to AI.

How it works: Step 1: Write a prompt that articulates the specific goals of the summary. Load it into a custom GPT (prompt available at the shared Google Doc). Step 2: Write the first draft for three takeaways — before looking at AI-generated notes, write down the top three takeaways you personally find essential. This forces prioritization and reflection before comparing with AI output. Step 3: Focus on 'why' and 'how,' not just 'what' — go beyond documenting what happened by clarifying why key decisions were made and how they impact team priorities. Step 4: Adjust your approach based on the meeting — for recurring updates or straightforward topics, let AI handle note-taking; for strategic insights and nuanced conversations, PMs should spend more time editing. AI handles grunt work; PMs layer in strategic depth.

### 48-Hour Remote Team Problem-Solving Offsite (Nikita Miller)
A structured approach to breaking strategic deadlocks in distributed teams.

How it works: Steps include: 1) Wave the flag when async decision-making is too slow. 2) Create a tight, well-articulated agenda beforehand. 3) Meet for exactly 48 hours (two nights). 4) Build in extended lunches and dinners for camaraderie. 5) Give the data person the floor first to educate everyone so all start with the same baseline information.

### Five Tips for Leading Effective PM Team Meetings (Leading a PM team meeting - Issue 19)
A five-step checklist for setting up and running a productive PM team meeting

How it works: 1. Do you even need a PM team meeting? Don't do this meeting just to do it. Write out what problem you're trying to solve. Consider if there are more efficient ways to solve this problem.
2. Be crystal clear on the goal. Choose from Understanding, Alignment, Connection. Make goals explicit. Regularly check in on whether you're achieving them.
3. Meet often enough, but not too often. Suggested cadence: 1-2x per month. Bigger team = less frequent. Weekly becomes redundant with 1:1s. Less than monthly loses most benefits beyond social bonding. No laptops allowed.
4. Make it easy to find, and to add to, the meeting agenda. Use a shared doc linked in the invite. Let the team add items asynchronously. Small team: open access. Large team (12+): designate a point person.
5. Make it fun. PM team meetings tend to get super serious. Ideas: (a) Have each person share something fun they did recently, (b) Invite guest speakers, (c) Do a book club, (d) Have the meeting in an interesting location, (e) Go out for drinks afterward.

### Five Ways to Make PM Team Meetings Fun (Leading a PM team meeting - Issue 19)
A list of tactics to add lightheartedness and engagement to PM team meetings

How it works: 1. Have each person share something fun they did recently
2. Invite guest speakers
3. Do a book club

5. Go out for drinks afterward

### High-Value Meeting Process (Naomi Gleit)
A step-by-step checklist for running an effective, decision-oriented meeting.

How it works: 1. Send agenda and pre-read 24 hours prior. 2. Cancel meeting if pre-read isn't sent. 3. Project a visual during the meeting to anchor latecomers. 4. Present exactly 3 options and 1 recommendation. 5. Real-time edit the visual to document decisions. 6. Reply-all to the calendar invite with notes within 24 hours.

### Live-in-Meeting Action Item Checklist (Product manager is an unfair role. So work unfairly.)
A list of action items PMs can complete live during meetings while screensharing, instead of adding them to a post-meeting to-do list.

How it works: While screensharing during the meeting:
1. Draft meeting summary bullet points and action items in Slack (in the initiative's channel)
2. Ask attendees to review on screen: 'Let's make sure I got it right'
3. Hit 'enter' on Slack once consensus is reached
4. Write Jira tickets live (screenshare and say 'Let's make sure I got it right')
5. Ping a colleague for input (screenshare and say 'I'll try to quickly get a response while we're still in the meeting')
6. Schedule the next meeting (screenshare the 'find a time' screen: 'Let's make sure we get this on the calendar and move anything we need to')

### Meeting & Approval Limits (Paige Costello)
Hard constraints to prevent slow decision-making and bloated meetings.

How it works: Rule 1: Maximum of 3 blocking approvers on any given piece of work. Rule 2: Maximum of 10 people in a decision-making meeting (if more, kick people out and send better asynchronous notes).

### Meeting Effectiveness Checklist for PMs (Startup PM vs. big company PM)
A step-by-step checklist for running productive meetings as a product manager

How it works: **Before the meeting:**
- Include the goal of the meeting in the calendar invite
- Include an agenda in the calendar invite
- Invite as few people as possible

**During the meeting:**
- If the meeting doesn't feel productive, call it out
- Ensure you leave with clear action items

**After the meeting:**

### Meeting End Questions (Alisa Cohn)
Three questions to ask at the end of every meeting to ensure alignment and action.

How it works: 1. What did we decide here? 2. Who needs to do what by when? 3. Who else needs to know?

### Meeting Priming Checklist (Evan LaPointe)
A set of questions to answer at the start of a meeting (or in the invite) before moving into decision-making.

How it works: 1. What is the category of conversation? 2. What basic principles apply? 3. Are we honoring sacred cows or eating them? 4. What is the ultimate purpose and mindset required?

### Meeting Time Management Tactics (A PM’s guide to influence)
Techniques for managing the clock in executive decision meetings to ensure decisions are reached

How it works: Before the meeting:
- State a concrete desired outcome upfront: e.g. 'Our goal today is to get approval on our go-to-market plan for our upcoming launch.'
- This gives you legitimacy to steer conversation back to decisions later.

During the meeting:
- Use straightforward and concise language (as if talking to a teenager with no context in your area)
- To redirect tangents: 'Thanks for bringing this up. I recommend we take that issue offline or come back to it after we get through the critical decisions for today, since we only have 5 minutes left.'
- To refocus: 'Since we only have 8 minutes left, let's jump to the discussion about [timely issue], which we need to resolve today to avoid delaying launch.'

Adapt format to audience:
- For fast-processing execs (e.g. Slack CEO): Skip context slides, get to the crux immediately, keep attendance small for candid conversation
- For Google-style reviews: Use a memo format, ask execs to read and add written questions in first 10 minutes, then synthesize and address questions for remaining 20 minutes

Key risk: With senior executives, if you don't exit the meeting with a decision, you sometimes might not get ahold of them again for weeks or months.

### Post-Mortem Best Practices Checklist (Ideal sprint length, designer vs. PM roles, running PM team meetings, running post-mortems, best product/executive coaches, and much more)
A step-by-step checklist for running an effective, blameless post-launch post-mortem

How it works: Before the Meeting:
1. Circulate a document with key dates, events, and milestones so everyone shares the same factual baseline
2. Send a pre-meeting survey asking participants to reflect on what went well, what didn't, and what to improve
3. Give people ample time to think (a few days) — or plan silent time during the session
4. Distill survey responses into the top 3 key themes to focus discussion

During the Meeting:
5. Set the tone: Read Deming's quote — 'A bad system will beat a good person every time'
6. Emphasize: We're here to fix the SYSTEM, not blame people
7. Focus discussion on only the 3 distilled themes — trying to cover everything gets out of hand
8. Use a retrospective playbook to guide the session (e.g., Atlassian's retrospective play)
9. Use tools like stickies.io for remote retros
10. Maintain psychological safety — watch for defensiveness as a signal

Mindset for Leaders:
11. Come to the table assuming you have a high-performing team with positive intent
12. Look for systemic/process causes, not individual failures
13. Remember: Leader's preconceived notions impact outcomes (teams perform worse when leaders assume people are making mistakes)

Recommended reading: 'The Blame Bias' chapter from 'Primed to Perform' by Lindsay McGregor and Neel Doshi

### Scheduling Etiquette Rules (Sam Lessin)
How to handle meeting scheduling while respecting power dynamics and busyness hierarchies

How it works: 1) Don't default to Calendly — it signals you're pushing work to the other person, 2) If you're less senior/busy, let them pick the time and make it work, 3) Give real availability options (one of first three should work), 4) When rescheduling, give maximum notice and be even more accommodating, 5) Double-check time zones, 6) Schedule at reasonable hours for their time zone, 7) Treat EAs/PAs with deep respect — say thank you, follow up with them, 8) Never disrespect gatekeepers

### Arianna Huffington's Reset Ritual (Shishir Mehrotra)
A personal 1-minute breathing exercise video that teams share at the start of meetings to reset brain chemistry and learn about each other.

How it works: Each person creates a personal 1-minute video using a template: includes personal photos (kids, hometown), favorite quotes, set to personally chosen music. Meetings start by 'spinning the wheel' to randomly select someone, then playing their Reset video while everyone does a breathing exercise. Two benefits: brain chemistry reset for focus, and team bonding through personal sharing. The music people choose is often the most revealing element.
