## Alisa Cohn

**Insight:** Ending every meeting with structured questions about accountability and communication ensures that ideas are converted into actual progress.

**Tactical advice:**
- Ask three specific questions at the end of every meeting to confirm alignment on outcomes and next steps.
- Explicitly identify who is responsible for each action item to establish clear accountability before the meeting ends.
- Establish a communication plan for stakeholders who were not present to ensure the meeting's progress is shared effectively.

*Source: [Scripts for difficult conversations: Giving hard feedback, navigating defensiveness, the three questions you should end every meeting with, more | Alisa Cohn (executive coach)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=bvF0ZM8DjuI) @ 00:50:01*

## Annie Duke

> "People generally think the purpose of a meeting is for three things, discover, discuss, decide. The only thing that's ever supposed to happen in a meeting is the discussion part."

**Insight:** Maximize meeting efficiency by ensuring information discovery happens beforehand and final decisions happen afterward, leaving the meeting time solely for discussion.

**Tactical advice:**
- Complete the 'discovery' phase of information gathering asynchronously before the meeting.
- Use the actual meeting time strictly for discussing different perspectives and data.
- Separate information sharing from the final decision to prevent social pressure and the loudest voices from dominating.

*Source: [This will make you a better decision-maker | Annie Duke (author of “Thinking in Bets” and “Quit,” former pro poker player)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=svQMODvIGAE) @ 00:24:54*

## Lenny Rachitsky

> "Our PM team has a section carved out for “what feels hard [harder than it should be]?” The purpose of this section is to discuss sticking points up and down the stack: troubleshooting comms with Team X; doing a teardown of a PRD that has raised unexpected questions and gotten “stuck”; trying on versions of a Vision/Strategy."

**Insight:** The most effective PM team meetings prioritize psychological safety and vulnerability, creating a dedicated space for peers to help each other solve difficult systemic challenges.

**Tactical advice:**
- Implement a 'what feels hard' agenda item to encourage vulnerability and surface hidden sticking points across the product organization.
- Rotate a guest speaker slot each month where one PM presents a specific challenge or key decision for group deep-dive feedback.
- Start meetings by allowing anyone to speak to model open listening and build trust within the team.

> "Instead of adding action items from meetings to a to-do list, do the action items *live in the meeting*. With everyone watching. While screensharing."

**Insight:** Completing small tasks and achieving alignment during a meeting prevents the accumulation of post-meeting administrative debt.

**Tactical advice:**
- Draft meeting summaries and action items in a shared Slack channel while screensharing for live consensus.
- Write Jira tickets or schedule follow-up calls live during the meeting to ensure all details are correct.
- Hit 'enter' on summaries or pings before the meeting ends to clear the task from your plate immediately.
