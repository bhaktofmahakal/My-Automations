## Alisa Cohn

> "You know, Matilda, I want to chat with you about the way you're interacting with your peers. So what I'm hearing from them is that you're missing deadlines on a regular basis and not letting them know you're missing the deadlines, and that also you're not fully keeping your team up to speed. And so they're kind of confused running around. Now, we both know that the most important way you can be successful here and also achieve your goals is to make sure that you are working with your peers in a way that's consistent and that they can count on you and you can count on them."

**Insight:** Frame difficult feedback as a joint effort to help the employee achieve their own professional goals and improve team coordination.

**Tactical advice:**
- Use phrases like 'what I'm hearing' to cite feedback from multiple sources rather than making the observation feel like a personal attack.
- Connect the required behavior change directly to the employee's own desire for career success and goal achievement.
- Conclude the conversation by ensuring there is a clear plan for how the employee will change their specific behavior moving forward.

## Carole Robin

> "We don't understand that we are only privy to two out of the three, so I know what's going on for me and I know what I did. I have no idea what happened on your end."

**Insight:** Effective feedback requires acknowledging that you only have access to your own intent and the other person's behavior, while the actual impact on them must be discovered through dialogue.

**Tactical advice:**
- Distinguish between true emotional feelings and judgments disguised as "I feel" statements (e.g., "I feel you don't care").
- Stay on "your side of the net" by only speaking to what you know: your intent and the impact a behavior had on you.
- Inquire about the other person's reality rather than assuming you know their motivations or the impact of your actions.

*Source: [How to build deeper, more robust relationships | Carole Robin (Stanford GSB professor, “Touchy Feely”)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Cew9-GlC_yk) @ 00:44:00*

---

**Insight:** Damaged relationships are repaired by moving past distancing emotions like anger to share the more vulnerable, connecting emotions like hurt or fear that lie beneath.

**Tactical advice:**
- Identify when you are using anger as a "distancing emotion" to create space during a conflict.
- Look for the secondary emotions, such as fear or hurt, that are actually driving your defensive reaction.
- Share those connecting secondary emotions with the other person to bridge the distance created by the conflict.

## Varun Parmar

**Insight:** Cultivate leadership trust and growth by dedicating specific time for vulnerable, direct feedback from the team.

**Tactical advice:**
- Dedicate 1-2 hours at every leadership offsite for direct team feedback.
- Model vulnerability by openly receiving critiques in front of the leadership team.
- Use feedback sessions to identify personal growth areas and build cultural trust.

*Source: [An inside look at how Miro builds product: Lessons on outmaneuvering competitors, team structure, product quality, and moving fast | Varun Parmar (CPO of Miro)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=furNg4njlsg) @ 01:07:07*
